# Vertical brief: EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) for enterprise AI deployers (for Cato SEs)

Research date: 21 July 2026. Audience for the eventual page: organisations that USE third-party
AI (GenAI SaaS, embedded AI features, internal apps built on GPAI) — i.e. **deployers**, not AI
vendors. Every claim below carries a source; anything that could not be sourced was omitted.
NO customer references.

**DATE-CURRENCY WARNING**: this brief was researched days before **2 August 2026**, the AI Act's
general applicability date, and days after the "Digital Omnibus on AI" amending regulation
finished its legislative journey (signed but, as far as could be verified, **not yet published
in the Official Journal** at research time). The page must be written to read correctly both
before and after 2 Aug 2026 — use "from 2 August 2026" phrasing, never "in the coming weeks".

## 1. CRITICAL STATUS CHECK — the Digital Omnibus IS adopted; the 2 Aug 2026 high-risk

## deadline IS delayed (but Art 50 transparency is NOT)

- The Commission proposed the **"Digital Omnibus on AI"** (targeted amendments to the AI Act)
  on **19 November 2025**, citing delays in harmonised standards, national authority
  designation and conformity-assessment bodies.
  Sources: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/digital-omnibus-ai-regulation-proposal,
  https://www.europarl.europa.eu/legislative-train/package-digital-package/file-digital-omnibus-on-ai
- Legislative journey, verified: provisional **political agreement 7 May 2026** (trilogue);
  **European Parliament formal adoption 16 June 2026**; **Council final approval 29 June
  2026**; entry into force on the **third day after publication** in the Official Journal
  (publication had not yet been verified as of this research date — see unresolved questions).
  Sources: https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/05/EUs-Digital-Omnibus-on-AI-7-Key-Changes-You-Need-to-Know,
  https://knowledge.dlapiper.com/dlapiperknowledge/globalemploymentlatestdevelopments/2026/The-Digital-AI-Omnibus-Proposed-deferral-of-high-risk-AI-obligations-under-the-AI-Act,
  https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2026/06/29/artificial-intelligence-council-gives-final-green-light-to-simplify-and-streamline-rules/
- **What the omnibus changes** (per law-firm analyses of the final text):
  - **Annex III stand-alone high-risk obligations** (employment screening, creditworthiness,
    education, essential services etc., incl. the Art 26 deployer duties for those systems):
    deferred from 2 Aug 2026 to **2 December 2027**.
  - **Annex I embedded high-risk** (AI in regulated products — medical devices, machinery,
    toys): deferred from 2 Aug 2027 to **2 August 2028**.
  - **Art 50(2) machine-readable marking of synthetic content**: grace period to
    **2 December 2026**, but only for systems placed on the market before 2 Aug 2026; all
    **other Art 50 transparency obligations still apply from 2 August 2026**.
  - **Art 4 AI literacy softened**: from "ensure a sufficient level of AI literacy" to
    "take measures to **support the development of** AI literacy" among staff.
  - **New Art 5 prohibition**: AI systems generating non-consensual intimate imagery or CSAM,
    with technical-safeguard compliance by **2 December 2026**.
  - **National AI regulatory sandboxes**: deadline moved to **2 August 2027**.
  - **AI Office powers centralised/expanded** (exclusive competence over GPAI-based systems
    and AI in VLOPs/VLOSEs; investigation and inspection powers).
  - Registration simplification for providers self-assessing systems as non-high-risk;
    special-category data processing for **bias detection extended to all providers AND
    deployers** under strict necessity; relief for "small mid-caps" (<750 staff / <€150M).
  Sources: https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/,
  https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/technology-quotient/eu-ai-act-unpacked-34-the-final-digital-omnibus-on-ai-key-amendments-to-the-a-102nber,
  https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/05/EUs-Digital-Omnibus-on-AI-7-Key-Changes-You-Need-to-Know,
  https://www.joneswalker.com/en/insights/blogs/ai-law-blog/yes-august-2-still-matters-the-eu-approved-a-high-risk-ai-delay-but-most-trans.html
- Nuance for honest framing: the Commission's original proposal tied high-risk application to
  a Commission confirmation that standards/support tools are available, with 2 Dec 2027 /
  2 Aug 2028 as backstops ("at the latest"); at least one analysis of the agreed text says the
  new dates apply "regardless of whether harmonised standards and Commission guidance are
  ready". Treat the fixed dates as the safe public statement; see unresolved questions.
  Sources: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/digital-omnibus-ai-regulation-proposal,
  https://verifywise.ai/blog/eu-ai-act-omnibus-what-changed
- SE takeaway (Jones Walker's framing): "August 2 still matters" — transparency, penalties and

## 3. Roles — and when a deployer becomes a provider

- The Act distributes duties across **providers** (develop/place on market), **deployers**
  (use an AI system under their authority in a professional context), **importers** and
  **distributors** (Art 3 definitions). The page's audience is deployers.
  Source: https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/3/
- **Art 25(1) role-shift**: a deployer/distributor/importer becomes the **provider** of a
  high-risk system (inheriting Art 16 provider duties incl. conformity assessment) if they
  (a) put their **name or trademark** on it, (b) make a **substantial modification** to it
  while it remains high-risk, or (c) **modify the intended purpose** of a non-high-risk
  system so it becomes high-risk. The original provider must cooperate/hand over technical
  information unless it excluded such modification.
  Source: https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/25/
- SE takeaway: an enterprise that white-labels or heavily customises a third-party AI system
  can silently cross from "deployer" into "provider" territory — a governance question no
  network product answers, but a strong reason to know exactly which AI is in use (discovery).

## 4. Scope and the UK angle (Art 2)

- Art 2(1) covers providers placing AI on the EU market wherever established; **deployers
  established/located in the EU**; and — Art 2(1)(c) — "providers and deployers of AI systems
  that have their place of establishment or are located in a third country, **where the
  output produced by the AI system is used in the Union**".
  Source: https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/2/
- Exclusions: military/defence/national security (Art 2(3)); scientific R&D (Art 2(6));
  purely personal non-professional use (Art 2(10)); free/open-source AI unless high-risk or
  caught by Art 5/Art 50 (Art 2(12)). Source: https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/2/
- **UK**: the Act does not apply domestically in the UK, but UK firms are in scope where they
  place AI on the EU market, put it into service in the EU, **or where their AI system's
  output is used in the EU** — the output-based trigger is the one that catches UK
  enterprises using AI on EU customers/staff.
  Sources: https://www.williamfry.com/knowledge/a-practical-guide-to-the-extraterritorial-reach-of-the-ai-act/,
  https://www.whitecase.com/insight-our-thinking/eu-ai-acts-extraterritorial-scope-part-2,
  https://www.isms.online/iso-42001/does-the-eu-ai-act-apply-to-the-uk/

## 5. Deployer obligations (the mappable core)

### 5.1 AI literacy — Art 4 (live since Feb 2025; enforced from Aug 2026)

- Applied from 2 Feb 2025; as amended by the omnibus, providers and deployers must "take
  measures to **support the development of** AI literacy" among staff and others operating
  AI on their behalf (softened from "ensure a sufficient level").
  Sources: https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/113/,
  https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/
- The Commission published an **AI Literacy Q&A (May 2025)** — a living document — and
  maintains a **living repository** of 40+ literacy practices from AI Pact signatories;
  supervision/enforcement starts 2 Aug 2026.
  Sources: https://www.traverssmith.com/knowledge/knowledge-container/the-eu-ai-acts-ai-literacy-requirement-key-considerations/,
  https://digital-skills-jobs.europa.eu/en/latest/news/share-your-example-ai-literacy-practice-ai-office,
  https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/ai-talent-skills-and-literacy

### 5.2 Prohibited practices — Art 5 (live since Feb 2025; the deployer-relevant ones)

- Eight prohibitions apply since 2 Feb 2025; most enterprise-relevant for deployers:
  **(e) untargeted scraping** of facial images from internet/CCTV to build facial-recognition
  databases; **(f) emotion inference in the workplace and education institutions** (narrow
  medical/safety exceptions); plus manipulation, exploitation of vulnerabilities, social
  scoring, profiling-only predictive policing, biometric categorisation of sensitive traits,
  and constrained real-time remote biometric ID.
  Source: https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/5/
- Commission **guidelines on prohibited practices approved 4 February 2025** (non-binding).
  Key deployer points: the workplace emotion-recognition ban binds the **deployer regardless
  of the provider's contractual exclusions**; the scraping ban is specific to facial images
  for recognition databases (voice scraping and non-recognition facial datasets are outside
  the prohibition).
  Sources: https://www.wsgrdataadvisor.com/2025/02/eu-commission-issues-guidelines-on-prohibited-ai-practices-under-eu-ai-act/,
  https://www.insideprivacy.com/artificial-intelligence/european-commission-guidelines-on-prohibited-ai-practices-under-the-eu-artificial-intelligence-act/,
  https://fpf.org/blog/red-lines-under-eu-ai-act-unpacking-the-prohibition-of-emotion-recognition-in-the-workplace-and-education-institutions/
- The omnibus adds the NCII/CSAM-generation prohibition (safeguards due 2 Dec 2026) — covers
  systems where such generation is "a reasonably foreseeable and reproducible outcome".
  Source: https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/

### 5.3 High-risk deployer duties — Art 26 (now applying from 2 Dec 2027 for Annex III)

Per Art 26, deployers of high-risk AI systems must:
- **use the system per the provider's instructions for use**;
- assign **human oversight** to persons with competence, training, authority and support;
- ensure **input data** under their control is "relevant and sufficiently representative in
  view of the intended purpose";
- **monitor operation** and inform the provider/authorities of risks and serious incidents;
- **keep automatically generated logs** "for a period appropriate to the intended purpose,
  **of at least six months**" (unless other EU/national law sets more);
- **inform workers' representatives and affected workers** before workplace use;
- (public bodies) verify EU-database registration; use provider info for **DPIA**s; inform
  natural persons subject to decisions; cooperate with authorities.
Source: https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/26/
- Certain deployers (public bodies, private providers of public services, some financial
  uses) must also run a **fundamental rights impact assessment** before first use (Art 27).
  Source: https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/27/
- Timing: these duties were to bite 2 Aug 2026 for Annex III; the omnibus moved that to
  **2 Dec 2027** (Annex I products: 2 Aug 2028). Sources: §1.
  (SE framing: the runway is for building the evidence machine — inventory, logging, oversight
  — not for ignoring it.)

### 5.4 Transparency — Art 50 (live 2 Aug 2026 — this is the imminent deployer-facing wave)

- **Provider duties**: disclose AI interaction unless obvious; mark synthetic audio/image/
  video/text machine-readably as artificially generated.
- **Deployer duties**: inform exposed persons when operating **emotion-recognition or
  biometric-categorisation** systems (plus GDPR compliance); **disclose deepfakes**
  (artistic/satirical works get lighter treatment); disclose AI-generated/manipulated **text
  published to inform the public on matters of public interest** unless under human
  editorial control/review.
  Source: https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/
- Breaches sit in the €15M/3% tier (see §6); Art 50 obligations apply from 2 Aug 2026 with
  only the legacy-system marking grace to 2 Dec 2026.
  Sources: https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/99/,
  https://www.joneswalker.com/en/insights/blogs/ai-law-blog/yes-august-2-still-matters-the-eu-approved-a-high-risk-ai-delay-but-most-trans.html

### 5.5 GPAI — what falls on deployers: essentially nothing directly

- Chapter V obligations (technical documentation, downstream information, copyright policy,
  training-data summary) fall on **providers of GPAI models only**, applying since 2 Aug
  2025. An enterprise using ChatGPT/Copilot/Gemini has no Chapter V duties; it meets GPAI
  through Art 50 transparency, Art 4 literacy, and Art 26 if a GPAI-based system is used in
  a high-risk context. Source: https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/53/
- Omnibus note: AI Office supervision extends to systems built on a GPAI model by the same
  undertaking and to AI in VLOPs/VLOSEs — still a provider/platform matter, not a deployer
  duty. Source: https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/technology-quotient/eu-ai-act-unpacked-34-the-final-digital-omnibus-on-ai-key-amendments-to-the-a-102nber

## 6. Penalties (verified against Art 99/101)

- **€35M or 7% of worldwide annual turnover** (whichever higher) — Art 5 prohibited
  practices.
- **€15M or 3%** — breaches of listed operator obligations including **Art 26 (deployers)**
  and **Art 50 (transparency)**.
- **€7.5M or 1%** — supplying incorrect/misleading information to authorities.
- SMEs/startups: the **lower** of the amount or percentage applies. Penalties chapter has
  applied since 2 Aug 2025 (except Art 101).
  Source: https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/99/
- **Art 101**: Commission fines on GPAI model providers up to **3% of global turnover or
  €15M**, applicable from 2 Aug 2026. Source: https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/101/

## 7. Governance and enforcement readiness

- **EU AI Office** (Commission) supervises GPAI (since Aug 2025); omnibus centralises and
  extends its powers (exclusive competence over GPAI-based systems and AI in VLOPs/VLOSEs;
  investigation and on-site inspection powers).
  Sources: https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/,
  https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/technology-quotient/eu-ai-act-unpacked-34-the-final-digital-omnibus-on-ai-key-amendments-to-the-a-102nber
- **National competent authorities** (market surveillance + notifying) were due by
  **2 Aug 2025**; per the AI Act Explorer implementation tracker (updated 17 June 2026):
  only **9 member states "clear"** (both authorities designated: Cyprus, Denmark, Finland,
  Hungary, Ireland, Italy, Lithuania, Malta, Slovenia), **12 partial**, **6 with no
  designation** — i.e. most member states missed the deadline and several still lack an
  enforcer weeks before general applicability. All 27 designated fundamental-rights
  authorities (Art 77, due Nov 2024).
  Source: https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/national-implementation-plans/
- Authority-designation delay was one of the Commission's stated reasons for the omnibus
  deferral. Source: https://knowledge.dlapiper.com/dlapiperknowledge/globalemploymentlatestdevelopments/2026/The-Digital-AI-Omnibus-Proposed-deferral-of-high-risk-AI-obligations-under-the-AI-Act
- **No landmark AI Act fine could be verified as of this research date** — do not claim
  enforcement actions on the page (see unresolved questions).

## 9. What network/SASE genuinely maps — and what it cannot do

### Can (with Cato-verified capability sources)

- **Shadow-AI inventory (maps to: knowing what you deploy — precondition for risk
  classification, Art 26 scoping, Art 4 scoping)**: Cato Shadow AI Discovery identifies AI
  services at network level via "application signatures, domains, and behavioral patterns",
  including **embedded AI features (e.g. Microsoft Copilot configurations)**, with user/
  device context, frequency/volume and risk attributes — explicitly framed as the discovery
  phase preceding policy enforcement. It "does not block traffic by default, and it does not
  inspect the content of AI prompts or responses".
  Source: https://knowledge.catonetworks.com/docs/what-is-shadow-ai-discovery.md
- **GenAI usage analytics**: the GenAI Apps Dashboard shows how many GenAI tools are used,
  sanctioned vs unsanctioned apps, risk-scored applications, top users, data violations and
  file uploads per app. Source: https://knowledge.catonetworks.com/docs/using-the-genai-apps-dashboard.md
  (plus GenAI reports: https://knowledge.catonetworks.com/docs/generating-a-genai-report.md)
- **Prompt-level policy enforcement (maps to: input-data discipline supporting Art 26(c),
  acceptable-use enforcement, preventing use of banned tools/features)**: the AI Security
  **User Interaction Policy** inspects prompts to AI chat apps in real time with actions
  **Block / Monitor / Anonymize-and-Block / Anonymize-and-Monitor**, DLP-style detectors,
  per-user/group, per-app granularity, user notification templates, and event logging.
  Requires TLS inspection. Source: https://knowledge.catonetworks.com/docs/working-with-the-user-interaction-policy.md
- **DLP monitoring of AI apps**: recommended DLP configuration for monitoring AI apps.
  Source: https://knowledge.catonetworks.com/docs/recommended-dlp-configuration-to-monitor-ai-apps.md

### Honest mapping to specific articles

- **Art 4 (literacy)**: network can *support* (real-time user warnings/notifications on risky
  AI use are a teachable-moment mechanism) but literacy is training/HR territory.
- **Art 5 (prohibitions)**: network can **block access** to services/categories the
  organisation has classified as prohibited-use (e.g. an emotion-recognition SaaS aimed at
  the workforce) and evidence non-use — but the legal classification of a practice as
  prohibited is a governance/legal call.
- **Art 26**: network contributes to *monitoring use* and *restricting who can reach which
  AI system* (oversight support), and prompt DLP disciplines *what goes in*; it does NOT
  satisfy instructions-for-use compliance, human-oversight arrangements, the system-log
  retention duty, worker consultation or registration.
- **Art 50**: network **cannot** label deepfakes or disclose AI interaction on the
  organisation's behalf — pure organisational duty; network evidence of which
  content-generating tools are in use helps scope where labelling processes are needed.
- **Never claimable**: risk classification, FRIA (Art 27), conformity assessment, technical
  documentation, DPIAs. Position as "Cato gives the visibility, control and evidence layer;
  the AI-governance programme is the customer's" — mirroring the library's "supports, never
  certifies" motif.
