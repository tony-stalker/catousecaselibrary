## 1. The standard

**Identity.** ISO/IEC 27001:2022, "Information security, cybersecurity and privacy protection —
Information security management systems — Requirements". Third edition, October 2022; amended by
Amd 1:2024 ("Climate action changes", February 2024), which adds a climate-change consideration to
Clause 4.1 and a note to Clause 4.2 — VERIFIED ([iso.org Amd 1 catalogue page](https://www.iso.org/standard/88435.html),
accessed 24 Sep 2026; [hightable.io amendment guide](https://hightable.io/iso270012022-amendment-1-absolutely-everything-you-need-to-know/),
accessed 24 Sep 2026). The base standard's catalogue page https://www.iso.org/standard/27001 returned
HTTP 403 to automated fetch on 24 Sep 2026; identity corroborated via the Amd 1 page and the secondary
sources below.

**Two halves — the page must keep them apart.**

- **Clauses 4–10 are the ISMS requirements** — 4 Context of the organisation, 5 Leadership, 6 Planning,
  7 Support, 8 Operation, 9 Performance evaluation, 10 Improvement. All use "shall"; none can be excluded.
  The 2022 revision added Clause 6.3 "Planning of changes" — VERIFIED
  ([secureframe.com clauses](https://secureframe.com/hub/iso-27001/clauses),
  [amtivo.com requirements guide](https://amtivo.com/us/resources/insights/iso-27001-requirements-a-comprehensive-guide/),
  [hightable.io clauses](https://hightable.io/iso-27001-clauses/), all accessed 24 Sep 2026).
- **Annex A is 93 reference controls in 4 themes** (2013 edition: 114 controls in 14 domains):
  A.5 Organizational — 37 (5.1–5.37); A.6 People — 8 (6.1–6.8); A.7 Physical — 14 (7.1–7.14);
  A.8 Technological — 34 (8.1–8.34). Annex A mirrors ISO/IEC 27002:2022, which holds the implementation
  guidance — VERIFIED ([scrut.io controls](https://www.scrut.io/hub/iso-27001/iso-27001-controls),
  [drata.com Annex A guide](https://drata.com/learn/iso-27001/controls-annex-a),
  [secureframe.com controls](https://secureframe.com/hub/iso-27001/controls), all accessed 24 Sep 2026).

**Eleven controls are new in 2022** — A.5.7 Threat intelligence, **A.5.23 Information security for use
of cloud services**, A.5.30 ICT readiness for business continuity, A.7.4 Physical security monitoring,
**A.8.9 Configuration management**, A.8.10 Information deletion, A.8.11 Data masking, **A.8.12 Data
leakage prevention**, **A.8.16 Monitoring activities**, **A.8.23 Web filtering**, A.8.28 Secure coding —
VERIFIED ([advisera.com 11 new controls](https://advisera.com/27001academy/explanation-of-11-new-iso-27001-2022-controls/),
[secureframe.com 2022 blog](https://secureframe.com/blog/iso-27001-2022), both accessed 24 Sep 2026).
The five bolded controls are core SASE capabilities — the page's "why now".

**Statement of Applicability (SoA).** Required by Clause 6.1.3(d): controls are selected via risk
assessment and treatment; the SoA records which Annex A controls apply, which are excluded, the
justification, and implementation status. Annex A is a reference set, not a mandatory checklist —
VERIFIED ([scrut.io](https://www.scrut.io/hub/iso-27001/iso-27001-controls),
[sprinto.com controls](https://sprinto.com/iso-27001/controls/), both accessed 24 Sep 2026).

**The 2013→2022 transition has closed.** Under IAF MD 26 the window ended **31 October 2025**; every
accredited ISO/IEC 27001:2013 certificate expired or was withdrawn on that date regardless of the printed
expiry — VERIFIED ([coalfire.com deadline countdown](https://coalfire.com/the-coalfire-blog/only-21-days-to-the-iso-270012013-expiration-deadline),
[infosecstandards.org news](https://infosecstandards.org/news/iso-27001-2013-certificates-expired), both
accessed 24 Sep 2026). Consequence framing — a lapsed organisation cannot revive a 2013 certificate and
must start a fresh Stage 1 + Stage 2 against 2022 — REPORTED (same sources' analysis). Every live
certificate a prospect meets in 2026 is a 2022-edition certificate; the page uses 2022 numbering only.

**Precision rule for the page:** the correct claim shape is "supports / helps implement and evidence
control X", never "makes you compliant with X" — applicability of any Annex A control is decided by the
customer's risk assessment and recorded in their SoA.

## 2. The certification journey and what auditors accept

Sequence and cycle, corroborated across a UKAS-accredited certification body (ISOQAR) and compliance
platforms — VERIFIED unless noted ([isoqar.com audit guide](https://isoqar.com/iso-standards/iso-27001/audit/),
[secureframe.com certification process](https://secureframe.com/hub/iso-27001/certification-process),
[vanta.com audit guide](https://www.vanta.com/collection/iso-27001/iso-27001-audit),
[iseoblue.com certification process](https://iseoblue.com/iso-27001/certification-guides/certification-process/),
all accessed 24 Sep 2026):

1. **Scope the ISMS** (Clause 4.3).
2. **Risk assessment and treatment** (6.1.2/6.1.3) → control selection.
3. **Produce the SoA, implement controls, run the ISMS** long enough to generate records — auditors
   expect it operating, typically ≥3 months, before Stage 2 — REPORTED ([iseoblue.com](https://iseoblue.com/iso-27001/certification-guides/certification-process/), accessed 24 Sep 2026).
4. **Stage 1 audit** — documentation/readiness review.
5. **Stage 2 audit** — typically 6–8 weeks later: auditors test whether controls are followed in
   practice — process walkthroughs, interviews, technical evidence; "the operational reality of the ISMS,
   not just its documented intent".
6. **Certificate valid 3 years**, annual **surveillance audits** in years 1 and 2, **recertification**
   in year 3.

**Evidence auditors accept:** documented policies; risk records; access-control records; monitoring logs
*plus proof they are reviewed* ("it's not enough to simply store logs — you must prove they are reviewed,
anomalies are handled, and outcomes are recorded"); incident reports; training records;
change/configuration records; consistent operation over time rather than an audit-day snapshot. Commonly
cited guidance is to hold **~12 months of logs** — REPORTED, practitioner guidance not standard text
([konfirmity.com evidence requirements](https://www.konfirmity.com/blog/iso-27001-evidence-requirements),
[bemopro.com audit logging](https://www.bemopro.com/compliance-requirements/iso-27001-audit-logging),
[hoop.dev audit logs](https://hoop.dev/blog/why-audit-logs-matter-for-iso-27001), all accessed 24 Sep 2026).

**UK angle:** UK procurement (NHS, defence, government) expects certification from a **UKAS-accredited**
body — REPORTED ([naqcyber.com UK guide](https://www.naqcyber.com/blog/iso-27001-certification-uk-business-guide-2026), accessed 24 Sep 2026).

**Market colour (one-liners, use sparingly):**
- 96,709 valid ISO/IEC 27001 certificates covering 179,877 sites in the ISO Survey 2024 — VERIFIED, with
  the caveat that 2024 was the first survey compiled from the IAF CertSearch database, which is why the
  count roughly doubled from 48,671 in 2023 — never present the jump as organic growth
  ([globalstd.com ISO Survey 2024 infographic](https://www.globalstd.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Infografia-ISO-Survey-2024_EN.pdf),
  [heic.eu analysis](https://heic.eu/insights/iso-27001-certifications-nearly-double-in-2024-as-global-organizations-prioritize-cybersecurity/), both accessed 24 Sep 2026).
- UK: 4,455 valid certificates, 4th worldwide behind China (33,359), India (6,758), Japan (6,644) —
  REPORTED ([compliancedocshq.com](https://compliancedocshq.com/learn/iso-27001-certification-statistics),
  globalstd.com infographic above, both accessed 24 Sep 2026).
- Timeline 3–12 months to certification for UK organisations; SMEs commonly 6–12 — REPORTED
  ([naqcyber.com](https://www.naqcyber.com/blog/iso-27001-certification-uk-business-guide-2026),
  [surecloud.com](https://www.surecloud.com/blog-hub/iso-27001-certification-cost-in-the-uk), both accessed 24 Sep 2026).
- Cost (UK) roughly £10k–£40k over a 3-year cycle; surveillance ~£2k–£4k/year — REPORTED, consultancy
  figures ([surecloud.com](https://www.surecloud.com/blog-hub/iso-27001-certification-cost-in-the-uk),
  [hightable.io cost guide](https://hightable.io/iso-27001-certification-cost/), both accessed 24 Sep 2026).

## 3. Annex A controls a SASE platform plausibly supports (2022 numbers and names)

([isms.online/iso-27001/annex-a/](https://www.isms.online/iso-27001/annex-a/), accessed 24 Sep 2026) —
every control below exists with that number in the 2022 edition. Names as listed (title case; the
standard prints them sentence-case). NEW = one of the 11 controls introduced in 2022 (§1). "Auditor looks
for" lines are synthesised from published control guidance (ISMS.online per-control pages; the A.8.16
page fetched directly — [isms.online 8.16](https://www.isms.online/iso-27001/annex-a/8-16-monitoring-activities-2022/),
accessed 24 Sep 2026) plus §2 — guidance-derived, not ISO/IEC 27002 quotations.

|---|---|---|---|

| A.5.15 | Access Control | Topic-level policy mapped to enforcement points; periodic review records | Client Connectivity Policy + WAN/Internet Firewall rulebases; Audit Trail of reviews/changes |

| A.6.7 | Remote Working | Remote-working policy + demonstrable technical protection of remote access + session logs | Always-On + posture-gated Client Connectivity; Client events |

| A.8.3 | Information Access Restriction | Enforced access rules, denied-access logs, reviews | WAN Firewall allowlist + implicit deny; block events |
| A.8.5 | Secure Authentication | MFA configuration, auth logs, lockout behaviour | Admin MFA/SSO; SSO in access path; Device Certificate check |

## 4. Device Posture — the anchor (all VERIFIED against the KB, 24 Sep 2026)

### 4.1 Building blocks: Device Checks → Device Posture Profiles

KB: **"Creating Device Posture Profiles and Device Checks"** —
https://knowledge.catonetworks.com/docs/creating-device-posture-profiles-and-device-checks (accessed 24 Sep 2026).

- Device Checks are individual requirements; Device Posture Profiles bundle them. Checks inside one
  profile combine with **AND**. Multiple profiles on one Client Connectivity rule combine with an implicit
  **OR** — "If multiple Profiles are included in a single Policy rule, there is an implicit OR between them"
  (**"Configuring the Client Connectivity Policy"**,
  https://knowledge.catonetworks.com/docs/configuring-the-client-connectivity-policy, accessed 24 Sep 2026).
- "Device Profiles can be added to the Client Connectivity Policy to determine which devices are allowed
  to connect to the network", and also to the "Internet and WAN firewall to create rules that include
  conditional access based on the actual device".
- **CMA path (new nav):** "From the navigation menu, select **Resources > Device Posture**" → **Device
  Checks** tab or **Device Posture Profiles** tab → **New**. Matches the library capture `cma-acc-posture.png`
  (three tabs: Device Posture Profiles, Device Checks, **Check Policy**; Device Posture sits under the
  OBJECTS group of the Resources menu).

- **Twelfth row in the KB table** (re-verified 24 Sep 2026 from the raw `.md` variant): "Device Checks
  applied for users in an office" — Windows 5.7, macOS 5.8, Linux 5.3. It is a capability, not a check

  devices connecting to your network behind a Socket. This lets you apply the same Device Posture

- Third-party agent checks (Anti-Malware, Firewall, Patch Management, DLP) are matched via **OPSWAT** —
  "The Cato Client uses the following versions of OPSWAT" with per-Client-version mappings; "For a list of
  supported vendors and products, see the drop-down lists in the **Vendor** section". Library capture
  `cma-posture-check-sophos.png` shows the vendor/product picker.
- Device Certificate check verifies a user certificate key pair installed on the device, signed and
  issued by a certificate authority associated with the account.
- **MDM signal as a check:** **"Configuring Intune MDM Compliance Checks for Device Posture"** —
  https://knowledge.catonetworks.com/docs/configuring-intune-mdm-compliance-checks-for-device-posture
  (accessed 24 Sep 2026): evaluates the device's Intune compliance state before allowing access; Windows,
  macOS (excluding multi-NIC devices) and Linux; created under the same Resources > Device Posture > Device
  Checks > New path and applied to new or existing posture profiles.

### 4.3 Where posture applies (enforcement points)

1. **Client Connectivity Policy** — "Configuring the Client Connectivity Policy" (URL in 4.1, accessed
   24 Sep 2026). CMA: **Access > Client Connectivity Policy**. Ordered rulebase; rules scope users/groups,
   countries, device OS, posture profiles; actions **"Allow WAN and Internet"**, **"Allow Internet only"**,
   **"Block WAN and Internet"**. "The device for any user or group that doesn't match any rule is blocked by
   the final implicit rule of the policy (ANY ANY block)."
2. **Internet and WAN Firewall rules** — **"Adding Device Conditions to Firewall Rules"**,
   https://knowledge.catonetworks.com/docs/adding-device-conditions-to-firewall-rules (accessed 24 Sep 2026).
   Device Posture Profiles and device attributes ("Category, Type, Model, OS, Manufacturer, OS Version")
   are matching criteria in the WAN and Internet firewall rulebases; attribute types AND together, values
   within a type OR. For Clients that cannot run a check you choose to "Skip the Device Check, and apply the
   firewall action to the unsupported Clients" or enforce it so the rule is skipped for them.
3. **Always-On** — **"Protecting Users with Always-On Security"**,
   https://knowledge.catonetworks.com/docs/protecting-users-with-always-on-security (accessed 24 Sep 2026).
   CMA: **Access > Always-On Policy**. "Your Client Connectivity Policy and Device Authentication settings
   take precedence over your Always-On Policy"; Device Posture Profiles can scope Always-On rules. Not
   supported for Linux Clients; bypass via admin one-time passcodes (valid up to 15 minutes) or
   user-supplied disconnect reasons.
4. **KB-recommended zero-trust pattern** — **"Zero Trust Device Security With Cato"**,
   https://knowledge.catonetworks.com/docs/zero-trust-device-security-with-cato (accessed 24 Sep 2026):
   prepare device certificates → "Configure a Device Check for the signing certificate and assign it to a
   Device Profile" → "Create a Client Connectivity policy rule that allows connections for devices that have
   the signing certificate installed"; "The final ANY ANY Block rule in the policy is applied to devices that
   don't have the certificate installed."

### 4.4 Continuous re-evaluation — the Check Policy

KB: **"Client Connectivity Policy - Continuous Posture Checks (Check Policy)"** —
https://knowledge.catonetworks.com/docs/client-connectivity-policy-continuous-posture-checks-check-policy
(accessed 24 Sep 2026).

- "The Check Policy defines how often the Client runs Device Posture Checks and when the checks are
  performed." CMA: **Resources > Device Posture > Check Policy tab**.
- Three modes: **"Continuous checks, even when disconnected (recommended)"**, **"Check on connect and
  while connected"**, **"One-time check"**. Recommended frequency **10 minutes**.
- Client support: **Windows Client v5.15+ and macOS Client v5.9+**.
- Default rollout and bounds: **"Upcoming Update to Device Posture Checks"** —
  https://knowledge.catonetworks.com/docs/upcoming-update-to-device-posture-checks (accessed 24 Sep 2026):
  "Starting August 7, 2026", continuous checks are "enabled by default" at "the recommended interval of
  every 10 minutes"; "minimum interval will be 5 minutes, and the maximum interval will be 1440 minutes
  (24 hours)", applying to "all accounts". As of 24 Sep 2026 continuous 10-minute checking is the platform
  default — sanity-check the tenant's Check Policy tab before demoing.

### 4.5 What happens on failure

From "Configuring the Client Connectivity Policy" (accessed 24 Sep 2026):

- At connect: "The Client does not connect to the Cato Cloud, and the Client shows an error message to the
  user"; the user can click **Details** to see the specific unmet requirements.
- While connected: "If a device fails a periodic check after the Client is connected, the Client
  disconnects" — same error with Details.
- Actions are allow/deny-shaped (Allow WAN and Internet / Allow Internet only / Block WAN and Internet).
  **No documented "warn-but-allow" action for posture failure in the Client Connectivity Policy.** The
  graduated middle ground is "Allow Internet only", or posture-scoped rules in the Internet/WAN firewall
  with the firewall's own actions.

### 4.6 Visibility of posture state

- **Events:** VERIFIED 24 Sep 2026 from the raw `.md` of "Configuring the Client Connectivity Policy",
  section "Understanding Events for the Client Connectivity Policy": "Click **Details** to show the
  specific requirements that the device doesn't meet. An event is also generated that shows the same
  details." The Block-action table lists **Event Sub-Type "Client Connectivity Policy"** with three
  reasons — "Device fails to meet the Device Check" ("Shows the details of the Anti-Malware or Firewall
  installed on the device, and what is required for the Device Check"), "Unsupported Client", and
  "Device fails to match any rule" (blocked by the final implicit rule). Allow-action events carry an
  "Authentication Method" field among others.
- **Events page:** **"Analyzing Events in Your Network"** —
  https://knowledge.catonetworks.com/docs/analyzing-events-in-your-network (accessed 24 Sep 2026). CMA:
  **Home > Events**. Also documents a **Posture** event type — "Events related to Posture checks" including
  "posture score changes and new checks" — which is about *account* posture (§5.1), not device posture.
- **Device Inventory:** **"What is Device Inventory?"** —
  https://knowledge.catonetworks.com/docs/what-is-device-inventory (accessed 24 Sep 2026). "Single
  dashboard that provides visibility for all devices that have connected to the network" at **Home >
  Devices > Inventory**, with per-device Quick View incl. an "Is Managed" flag.

## 5. Evidence-generation surfaces (all VERIFIED against the KB, 24 Sep 2026, unless labelled)

### 5.1 Posture Compliance Report and the Account Posture page — the headline find

KB: **"Generating Posture or Posture Compliance Reports"** —
https://knowledge.catonetworks.com/docs/en/generating-a-posture-or-posture-compliance-report (accessed 24 Sep 2026).

- The **Posture Report** "evaluates the configurations and settings in your account and shows how they
  comply with Cato's recommendations for optimal performance and security".
- The **Posture Compliance Report** "maps compliance controls from leading compliance frameworks" —
  **GDPR, ISO 27001:2022, and NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5** — "to the relevant Cato posture checks", to
  "understand compliance coverage, identify gaps, and prioritize remediation". Generated from **Home >
  Reports**; recurring options are quoted by the KB as "every two minutes, daily, weekly, or monthly" — the
  "every two minutes" wording is the KB's own and reads like a documentation quirk; the page quotes
  daily/weekly/monthly only.
- Data source is the Account Posture / Best Practices page — **"Reviewing Posture Checks for Your
  Account"**, https://knowledge.catonetworks.com/docs/reviewing-posture-checks-for-your-account (accessed
  24 Sep 2026): an **Account Score** ("the current overall account rating for compliance with Cato posture,
  as well as how many checks were passed and failed"), score-over-time trend, a checks table (All / Account
  Posture / SaaS Security tabs), checks re-run "automatically every 24 hours", each check carrying a
  **Label** mapping to "ISO 27001:2022, NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5, and GDPR", a "Review & Resolve" wizard and
  an AI "Guide me" walkthrough. CMA: **Home > Posture** (overview) and **Resources > Posture Check
  Catalog**.
- **Vocabulary trap:** Cato uses "posture" for two things — **Device Posture** (endpoint checks, §4) and
  **Account Posture** (CMA-configuration best-practice checks, this section). The page labels them
  distinctly everywhere.

### 5.2 Audit Trail (change-management evidence)

**"Using the Audit Trail"** — https://knowledge.catonetworks.com/docs/using-the-audit-trail (accessed
24 Sep 2026). Records "configuration changes made to your account, whether by administrators or an API",
including "additions, deletions, and edits of items such as rules, configuration settings, and more", with
before/after field values for certain policies. "Supports showing data for the previous 12 months",
viewable "up to 3 months of items at one time"; changes "may take several minutes" to appear. CMA:
**Account > Audit Trail** (new nav).

### 5.4 Reports and scheduled reports

Cato Reports family (summary from Learning Center search results, 24 Sep 2026 — REPORTED at the
wording level; the Reports UI itself is in library captures cma-reports-catalog-hitcount.png /
cma-reports-generated-pdfs.png): one-time or recurring ("daily, weekly, or monthly") reports "stored in the
Cato Cloud", "automatically emailed or downloaded", from predefined or custom templates; **Home > Reports**,
Catalog tab. The Rule Hit Count report is covered on security-compliance.html:
https://knowledge.catonetworks.com/docs/generating-rule-hit-count-reports-for-security-and-network-policies.

### 5.5 Policy revisions and publish (change control by design)

**"Working with Policy Revisions"** — https://knowledge.catonetworks.com/docs/working-with-policy-revisions
(accessed 24 Sep 2026). Each admin edits "in their own private revision" (some policies allow "multiple

### 5.6 Admin access control: MFA, SSO, RBAC

- **"Authenticating Admins"** — https://knowledge.catonetworks.com/docs/authenticating-admins (accessed
  24 Sep 2026). Three methods: **SSO (recommended)** (provider configured in Access > Single-Sign On),
  **Cato credentials**, and **MFA** with an authenticator app when using Cato credentials — "enabled by
  default for new admins, and is always enabled for admins in accounts created after December 10th, 2023".
  Caveat: "MFA is only supported for Cato user credentials. When admins log in with SSO, you can't require
  them to enter an MFA code" — MFA then belongs to the IdP. Settings: **Account > Login Restrictions**,
  **Access > Single-Sign On**.
- **"Managing Admin Roles Using RBAC"** — https://knowledge.catonetworks.com/docs/managing-admin-roles-using-rbac
  (accessed 24 Sep 2026). Nine predefined roles: "Editor, Viewer, Network Admin, Security Admin, Access
  Admin, Regional Viewer, Restricted Viewer, Logistics Admin, AI Security Sensitive"; custom roles with
  page-level Edit/View-only/None and entity-level scoping (sites, users, advanced groups); limitation: "you
  can't set separate permissions for individual tabs and features within a page". Paths: **Account > Roles &
  Permissions**, **Account > Administrators**.
- **"Working with Users"** — https://knowledge.catonetworks.com/docs/working-with-users (accessed 24 Sep
  2026): the Users table at **Access > Users** has a **"Client version"** column ("shows the version the
  Client is running") and a **"Devices"** column ("each device and operating system that is used by a user
  to connect"); "This section can be helpful for security auditing purposes." Backs the page's
  troubleshooting advice to check Client versions there before blaming a check.

## 6. Supporting control enforcement — one paragraph each (VERIFIED, 24 Sep 2026)

- **TLS Inspection** — the min-version/cipher sentence lives in **"Configuring TLS Inspection Policy for
  the Account"**, https://knowledge.catonetworks.com/docs/configuring-tls-inspection-policy-for-the-account
  (re-verified 24 Sep 2026): "To block outdated and insecure TLS versions or prevent the use of weak
  cipher suites in encrypted traffic the TLS Inspection policy can enforce the minimum TLS protocol
  versions and cipher suite strength for traffic in your account"; rule setting **"TLS Version and Cipher
  Suites"**; CMA **Security > TLS Inspection**. (Earlier passes attributed this to "Best Practices for TLS
  Inspection", https://knowledge.catonetworks.com/docs/best-practices-for-tls-inspection — that article
  only says "Cato supports TLS versions 1.1, 1.2, and 1.3" and that inspection "is bypassed for Android,
  Linux, and unidentified operating systems"; corrected 24 Sep 2026.) Artefact: the TLS Inspection
  rulebase + min-version/cipher enforcement (A.8.24).
- **Anti-Malware / NGAM** — "Configuring the Anti-Malware Policy",
  https://knowledge.catonetworks.com/docs/configuring-the-anti-malware-policy. Engines "scan WAN and
  Internet traffic for potentially malicious files" (HTTP/HTTPS/FTP, files to 100 MB); per-verdict actions
  and "tracking options for the rules to generate alerts and email notifications". CMA: **Security >
  Anti-Malware** (A.8.7).
- **IPS** — "Configuring the IPS Policy", https://knowledge.catonetworks.com/docs/configuring-the-ips-policy.
  "The IPS engine uses hundreds of static threat feeds that take known vulnerability patterns and turn them
  into signatures" plus ML modules; modes **Block** and **Monitor** ("Generates events (shown in Home >
  Events)"). CMA: **Security > IPS** (A.8.16).
- **DLP** — "Creating the Data Control Policy",
  https://knowledge.catonetworks.com/docs/creating-the-data-control-policy. Rules "inspect how data and
  content is transferred and moved within and outside of your organization"; "over 350 data types in over
  30 countries and languages"; actions Allow/Block/Notify; "Data Control rules require that TLS Inspection
  is enabled to inspect the content". CMA: **Security > App & Data Inline** (A.8.12, A.5.14).
- **CASB** — "Managing the Application Control Policy",
  https://knowledge.catonetworks.com/docs/managing-the-application-control-policy. Per-app risk score 0–10
  and rules keyed on compliance attributes — "HIPAA, ISAE 3402, ISO 27001, PCI-DSS, SOC-1, SOC-2, SOC-3, SOX,
  and SSO"; actions Allow/Block/Notify; requires the CASB licence. CMA: **Security > App & Data Inline**
  (A.5.23).
- **WAN Firewall (segmentation)** — "What is the Cato WAN Firewall?",
  https://knowledge.catonetworks.com/docs/what-is-the-cato-wan-firewall. "Controls access to objects and
  entities in your Wide Area Network (WAN)" with "a whitelist approach" and "a default ANY-ANY block rule to
  drop all connections that are not explicitly allowed in the rulebase" (A.8.22, A.8.3); posture profiles as
  rule conditions (§4.3).
- **Internet Firewall (web filtering)** — "Managing the Internet Firewall Policy",
  https://knowledge.catonetworks.com/docs/managing-the-internet-firewall-policy. Ordered rules; actions
  "Allow, Block, Prompt"; optional tracking to "generate Events and Send Notification"; final implicit
  ANY ANY **Allow** (the Internet Firewall is allow-by-default, unlike the WAN Firewall). CMA: **Security >
  Internet Firewall** (A.8.23). **Match objects** (re-verified 24 Sep 2026, "Reference for Rule Objects",
  https://knowledge.catonetworks.com/docs/reference-for-rule-objects): Application, Custom Application,
  Application Category, Custom Category, Custom Service, Custom Service IP, **FQDN** ("An exact match of
  the fully qualified domain… Wildcards are not supported"), **Domain** ("A Registered Domain and matches
  all subdomains"), IP Range, Any. **"URL" is not a documented object type** — the page says
  category/domain/FQDN rules, not "URL rules" (corrected 24 Sep 2026).
- **Always-On** — see §4.3 (A.6.7): remote workers cannot opt out of the controls; bypasses are
  themselves controlled artefacts (OTP up to 15 minutes, logged disconnect reasons).
