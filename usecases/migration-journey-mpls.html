MPLS to Cato: Animated Phased Journey

Nobody stalls at the start of an MPLS migration, or at the end — they stall in the middle, unsure whether migrated and un-migrated sites can still reach each other. Business objective

The middle is where migrations stall

A WAN migration is never a single weekend. Circuits expire on different dates, so for months some sites sit on Cato while others are still on MPLS — and the only question that matters is whether they can all still talk to each other. They can, throughout. The walkthrough below steps through the five phases of a typical MPLS exit; for the routing tables line by line, see the full detail in MPLS to SASE Migration & Co-existence.

At each phase

What changes, and who can reach whom

PhaseWhat changesReachability during this phase
0 · Legacy Baseline — branches, the Manchester colo interconnect and the London DC all hub-and-spoke through the MPLS cloud; AWS eu-west-2 hangs off the DC. Full any-to-any over MPLS; cloud workloads reached via the DC; nothing on Cato yet.
1 · Hub bridge The first Socket goes in at the Manchester colo, beside the MPLS CE, and peers eBGP with it. The hub is dual-homed — backbone up, MPLS down. Unchanged for users — both paths live; the handoff advertises each side's prefixes to the other.
2 · DCs on-net London DC gains an HA Socket pair; AWS eu-west-2 gains a vSocket — the early, low-risk wins. Their legacy links fall dormant. Branches still on MPLS reach the DC apps and AWS across the Manchester handoff.
3 · Waves Bristol and Leeds cut over to the PoP in migration waves; the MPLS cloud dims to a single remaining site. Migrated sites are on-net end to end; Glasgow still reaches everything via the handoff.
4 · Retired Glasgow cuts over; the MPLS CE at the colo and the contract are decommissioned. Full any-to-any over the Cato backbone; one routing table.
Why reachability never breaks

The Manchester Socket peers eBGP with the MPLS CE router on the colo LAN, advertising the prefixes of every site still on MPLS into the Cato backbone — and the Cato-side prefixes back into MPLS. So a migrated site always has a route to an un-migrated one (across the handoff), and vice versa. No site is ever cut off, at any point in the migration. The full co-existence use case walks the two routing tables that make this work, line by line.
