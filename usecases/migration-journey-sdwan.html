SD-WAN to Cato: Animated Phased Journey

SD-WAN displacements rarely stall on whether Cato is better — they stall on one question in the room: how do both fabrics coexist mid-cutover without breaking routing? This is that answer as a walkthrough. Press play and watch the eBGP-hub journey unfold phase by phase — a parallel Socket at the London hub, the Slough DC and Azure vWAN hub brought on-net, branch waves with instant rollback, then the legacy overlay switched off. Business objective

The question that stalls the deal

A prospect running a legacy SD-WAN fabric — the same overlay steering every branch back to a regional hub — believes the destination. What they can't picture is the transition: the weeks when half the estate is on Cato and half is still on the incumbent overlay, and traffic has to flow cleanly between them without asymmetric routing tearing sessions apart.

The proven answer is the eBGP-hub co-existence pattern: stand a Cato Socket up in parallel at the hub site, hand routes between the two fabrics over eBGP, and let AS-path length anchor every site to its own hub so paths stay symmetric. Below, that pattern is animated end to end. It is deliberately generic — a hub site, two branches, an on-prem DC and an Azure cloud hub — so it maps onto any legacy SD-WAN overlay. The vendor-specific mechanics live in the Fortinet playbook and the routing options A–F in the Migration Methodology.

Phase by phase

What happens at each step

The same five phases, as a reference table — what changes on the ground, and what routing looks like while that phase is live.

PhaseWhat changesRouting during this phase
0 · Legacy overlay Baseline: Bristol and Glasgow, the Slough DC and even the Azure vWAN hub are all anchored to the legacy hub appliance at the London hub site. No Cato. Branch-to-app and branch-to-cloud traffic hairpins through the London hub over the overlay. Nothing to co-exist with yet.
1 · Parallel Socket at the hub A Cato Socket is stood up beside the legacy appliance at the London hub; the Cato PoP and backbone come online; an eBGP handoff peers the two fabrics. Legacy still carries all production. The fabrics exchange routes over eBGP — validated with test prefixes before a single site moves.
2 · DC and cloud on-net Slough DC comes up on HA Sockets and the Azure vWAN hub on a vSocket — the classic early, low-risk wins. Their overlay links fall dormant. Apps in Slough and Azure are reachable natively over the backbone; branches still on the overlay reach them across the hub eBGP handoff. Rollback re-enables the legacy links.
3 · Branch waves Bristol and Glasgow cut over in waves — legacy spoke disabled, Cato site brought up. The legacy hub empties out. Migrated sites are symmetric on Cato; anything still on the overlay is anchored to its own hub by AS-path length. The handoff stays as the rollback path until sign-off.
4 · Overlay retired The legacy hub appliance at London, its overlay links, controllers and licences are decommissioned. Every branch, the Slough DC and the Azure vWAN hub run on the Cato backbone. The eBGP handoff and the overlay are removed — one fabric remains.
The routing insight

Why routing stays symmetric throughout

The whole journey hangs on one property: while both fabrics are live, a flow and its return must take the same hub — otherwise the stateful firewalls on each side see only half the conversation and sessions drop. Three things hold that line.

🔌

A parallel Socket at the hub site

The Cato Socket sits alongside the legacy hub appliance at the London hub — the two fabrics meet in one place, so there is a single, controlled boundary between them rather than a mesh of interconnects. The hub site is the bridge; it need not be a datacentre.

🔁

An eBGP handoff between the fabrics

Routes are exchanged dynamically at that boundary. Migrating a site withdraws its prefixes from one fabric and advertises them into the other automatically — no per-site static route engineering during the window.

🧭

AS-path length anchors each site

Crossing between fabrics adds an AS hop, so best-path selection keeps a site's traffic — and its return — on its own regional hub. Symmetry is a property of the routing, not a manual pin.

↩️

Rollback is reconvergence

Every cutover reverses by re-enabling configuration that still exists: re-enable the legacy spoke and BGP reconverges. No rebuild, no truck roll — which is exactly what makes the phased approach safe to start.
