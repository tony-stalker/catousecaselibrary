Law Firms: Client Confidentiality & Ethical Walls

For a law firm, confidentiality is not a compliance line item — it is the product. Clients impose Outside Counsel Guidelines with security requirements and audit rights, conflicts demand ethical walls that actually hold, and lateral hires arrive and leave with matter access that must change the same day. This use case shows how one converged platform turns those obligations into enforceable, evidenced network policy: identity-based walls, DLP over privileged material, and an audit trail ready for the next client security questionnaire.

Business objective

Why this matters

A law firm sells two things: judgment and discretion. Privilege and confidentiality are existential — a single failure can lose a client, an injunction, or the firm's standing to act. In England and Wales the obligation is regulatory as well as commercial: the SRA Codes of Conduct (paragraph 6.3) require solicitors and firms to keep the affairs of current and former clients confidential, and paragraph 6.5 bars acting where a client's interest is adverse to another current or former client for whom the firm holds material confidential information — unless effective measures protect that information or informed consent is obtained. The courts set the bar for "effective" high: since Bolkiah v KPMG, an information barrier must leave no real risk of disclosure, and a real risk need not be substantial.

Commercial pressure now arrives faster than regulation. Sophisticated clients — banks, insurers, private equity — attach Outside Counsel Guidelines (OCGs) to their instructions: encryption standards, access-control expectations, breach-notification windows, restrictions on where matter data may live, and audit rights over the firm's security. Every panel review and new instruction brings another security questionnaire. The objective for the firm's COLP, IT director and general counsel is the same one, stated three ways: enforce confidentiality technically, not just contractually — and be able to prove it on demand.

The problem

Policy on paper, flat network in practice

Consider a fictional 400-lawyer, UK-headquartered firm with offices in London, Manchester and Dubai. Its confidentiality obligations are impeccably documented — and its estate undermines them four ways:

OCG sprawl and questionnaire fatigue

Dozens of clients, dozens of OCG variants, each with its own security schedule and audit rights. Every panel renewal means another questionnaire — and the answers live across firewall configs, VPN concentrators, DMS permissions and spreadsheets. Assembling evidence is a project each time, and partners feel it as lost fee-earning days.

Ethical walls that stop at the DMS

Walls are declared in the conflicts system and enforced in the document management system (iManage- or NetDocuments-class platforms). But the network underneath is flat: both matter teams sit on the same LAN and VPN, and nothing at the network layer distinguishes them. "Effective measures" with no real risk of disclosure is a hard claim when segregation exists in one application only.

Lateral churn on a same-day clock

Lateral hires arrive mid-matter and need access granted precisely — often behind a wall from day one because of prior-firm conflicts. Departures are worse: a leaver heading to opposing counsel must lose matter access the same day, and the firm must be able to show when it was revoked, not assert that it was.

Fee-earners work from everywhere

Courts, client sites, chambers, home, trains. Privileged material moves with them — on laptops, over hotel Wi-Fi, into personal cloud drives when deadlines bite. A VPN that drops users onto the flat network extends every weakness above to every location a fee-earner opens a lid.

Discovery questions

"How is an ethical wall enforced today below the DMS — what stops a fee-earner on Matter A reaching Matter B's workspace over the network?" · "How long does a client security questionnaire take to answer, and how many systems hold the evidence?" · "When a lateral leaves for opposing counsel, who proves — with timestamps — that access was revoked?"

The Cato solution

Ethical walls as network policy — enforced and evidenced

Cato makes the ethical wall a property of the network itself. Matter teams are represented as IdP groups, and a pair of WAN Firewall rules per wall — allow each team to its own matter's application segment, everything else falling to the implicit deny — enforces the barrier on every flow, wherever it originates. A fee-earner in the office, at court or at home crosses the same PoP-enforced policy, because the Cato Client applies identical rules to remote users. DLP inspects what leaves — blocking privileged material bound for personal cloud storage — and every decision, allow or block, lands in the Cato Management Application as the evidence a client auditor asks for.

Position with care

Cato enforces the wall at the network layer — it complements the DMS's own ethical-wall and matter-security features (in iManage- or NetDocuments-class systems), it does not replace them, and no product integration is implied. Whether the combined measures meet the SRA's "effective measures" bar is a judgment for the firm and its COLP. Position Cato as making that claim materially easier to defend: segregation enforced in two independent layers, with evidence.

Law-firm scenarios

  • A new conflict wall, stood up the same day. A fresh instruction throws up a conflict the moment it lands. Once the conflicts team declares the wall, you add a single rule pair scoped to the two matter IdP groups (rows 1–2 and their mirror), deployed monitor-first then enforced — the barrier exists at the network layer within the hour, a second independent layer beneath the DMS's own wall, not a change ticket waiting in a queue.
  • A client's OCG security questionnaire arrives. Rather than a firm-wide scavenger hunt, the evidence pack comes from one console: the rulebase as configured (walls, DLP, access), events showing those controls firing on real traffic, and the Audit Trail of every change — the raw material for most questionnaire sections, assembled in an afternoon instead of costing partners fee-earning days.
  • A lateral partner exits to opposing counsel. Remove them from the matter groups in the IdP and SCIM collapses their wall and ZTNA scope the same day — rows 1–5 simply stop matching. The Audit Trail and events then give the timestamped who and when of the revocation, and the history of what that identity could reach: evidence of a clean exit, not an assertion of one.
  • A deal-room period on a live transaction. While the deal is open, tighten the controls for the deal-team group — move row 6's DLP Legal profile from monitor to block on that group's traffic and keep row 7's GenAI block in force — then relax back to baseline at completion. Because scoping is by group, the heightened posture lands on the deal team without disturbing the rest of the firm, and the change window is recorded in the Audit Trail.
Demo runbook

How to run the demo

Before you demo — find the live wound

Ask which is currently hurting: a wall the firm cannot prove, a questionnaire eating partner time, or a lateral departure that got uncomfortable. Lead with that scene; the rest of the runbook supports it.

  1. Frame with the obligation map

    Walk the table above, including the "what stays with the firm" column. Agreeing what Cato does not do — conflicts decisions, DMS permissions — buys the credibility for everything that follows.

  2. Build a wall as a rule pair

    Security → WAN Firewall

    Create (or walk) two rules scoped to IdP groups: WALL-A → Matter A workspace segment, allow and WALL-B → Matter B workspace segment, allow — everything else falls to the implicit deny. Point at the source column: the wall is identity, so it holds in every office and for every remote user, automatically.

  3. Attempt cross-wall access — show the block

    Monitor → Events

    As a WALL-A user (say, Priya), attempt to reach the Matter B workspace — then filter events to her identity and show the block: rule name, user, time, source. Anchor: "this event is what 'effective measures' looks like as evidence, not assertion."

  4. Block a privileged upload with DLP

    Security → DLP Configuration

    Show a DLP rule scoped to fee-earner groups matching legal/contract document classifiers and firm-defined data types, blocking uploads to personal cloud storage. Trigger it with a sample privileged-style document and show the block event. Describe the scoping honestly — group- and data-type-based, not matter-aware.

  5. Revoke a leaver — same day, with proof

    Access → Users

    Remove a departing lateral (say, Marcus) from the matter groups in the IdP and show the change flow through via SCIM: his wall rules no longer match, his ZTNA scope collapses. The timestamped record answers "who proves access revocation" directly.

  6. Close on the questionnaire pack

    Administration → Audit Trail

    Show who changed which rule, when, from where — and export it. Pair with the rulebase and the event stream: policy, enforcement and change history from one console. "This is the client security questionnaire answering itself."

Prerequisites — agree before week one
  • The conflicts team nominates the wall. The pilot evidences a barrier the firm's information-barrier policy already governs — Cato does not define who is walled from what. Get the pair, and the COLP's awareness, in writing at the workshop.
  • Two addressable workspaces. The wall is network policy, so the two matter workspaces must be distinct WAN destinations — separate hosts, subnets or application segments. If both matters sit behind one DMS front-end on one address, agree the segment mapping (or a test-workspace pair) before week one, not mid-pilot.
  • Identity first, and flat. The matter-team groups sync from the firm's IdP via SCIM before any rule is written — and they must be flat: nested-group provisioning is not supported over SCIM, so flatten in the IdP (design patterns in Identity Integration Design).
  • Both teams on Cato end to end — the pilot office behind a socket and every pilot fee-earner on the Cato Client, so the office / home / court Wi-Fi criterion is an honest test of one policy, not three.
  • TLS inspection staged first for the DLP slice only. The wall proofs need no decryption; the Legal-profile slice does. Stage TLSi for the pilot cohort per TLS Inspection: Staged Rollout Playbook, with whoever owns privacy signing the scope.
  • Licences — ZTNA/SDP covering the pilot cohort for the period, and the DLP capability in scope for the monitor slice.

Configuration walkthrough

  1. Provision the matter-team groups

    Access → Directory Services Access → Users

    Sync two flat IdP groups — WALL-A and WALL-B — via SCIM and confirm the rosters under Users match the conflicts register exactly: Priya in WALL-A, Sofia in WALL-B, Marcus starting in WALL-A. Membership changes reflect in near real time once the IdP syncs, and every rule in this pilot references these groups — so the team-move drill later rides the same mechanism (SCIM User Provisioning).

  2. Author the wall — monitor-first

    Security → WAN Firewall

    Create the four rules of blueprint rows 1–4: each team allowed to its own workspace segment, and — for week one only — the two cross-wall rows set to Allow with tracking on Events rather than Block. Rules evaluate top-down, first-match, over the final implicit ANY ANY block, and take users and groups as source objects (Managing the WAN Firewall Policy · Reference for Rule Objects). The monitor week earns its keep twice: it proves the rules match the right traffic before anything blocks, and any cross-wall hit it records is a finding about today's flat network — reachability the current estate cannot even see.

  3. Enforce, then stage the cross-wall test

    Security → WAN Firewall Monitor → Events

    Start of week two: review the monitor week's events with the customer, then flip the two cross-wall rows to Block. Run the test that anchors the PoV: Priya opens her own Matter A workspace (allow, named rule), then attempts Matter B — blocked, attributed to her identity, dated. Sofia mirrors it in the other direction. Screenshot the pair: the barrier proven both ways, as evidence rather than assertion.

  4. Move the attempt, not the policy

    Monitor → Events

    Priya repeats the cross-wall attempt from home on the Cato Client, and again from court Wi-Fi. Same rule name, same block, three source contexts — filter events to her identity and show the three rows side by side. This is the criterion the flat-VPN estate cannot meet: the wall is enforced at the PoP, so location never re-decides policy.

  5. Add the Legal DLP slice — monitor only

    Security → DLP Configuration

    One Data Control rule scoped to the two pilot groups: uploads to personal cloud storage, matched against Cato's predefined Legal profile — ML classifiers for agreements, patents, court documents and powers of attorney — tracking events, blocking nothing (Recommended DLP Configuration to Monitor AI Apps · DLP Content Profiles). Trigger it with a fictional privileged-style test document, never a real one. Scoping is group- and data-type-based, not matter-aware — say so in the sync, exactly as the obligation map above does. The GenAI half of the data story belongs to Law Firms: GenAI with Privilege Intact — keep it out of this pilot's scope.

  6. Run the team-move drill

    Access → Users Monitor → Events

    Mid-pilot, the customer moves Marcus from WALL-A to WALL-B in the IdP — always at the IdP, never in Cato directly. Record three timestamps: the IdP change, his first blocked Matter A attempt, his first allowed Matter B access. Set expectations from the documentation, then measure: Entra ID initiates its automatic sync roughly every 40 minutes, and on-demand provisioning does not cover group assignment — so the window is the sync cycle, and the criterion is the number you record, not "instant" (SCIM Provisioning with Entra ID). The same drill, run with removal instead of a move, is the leaver story from the demo — one mechanism, evidenced twice.

  7. Assemble the per-matter evidence pack

    Monitor → Events Administration → Audit Trail

    Filter events by each matter workspace as destination and export: every identity that reached it, when, under which rule — a list that should read as the matter team's roster and nothing else. Add the cross-wall block pairs, the three-location set, the DLP monitor events and the Audit Trail of every policy change, dated into the evidence doc. At the wrap-up this pack goes to the COLP as the network layer's contribution to the firm's "effective measures" file — evidence for the firm's judgment, not a substitute for it.

Troubleshooting the pilot

🧭

Group roster doesn't match the register

Members missing from a synced group usually belong via a nested group — nested-group provisioning is not supported over SCIM. Flatten in the IdP so every member is direct, re-sync, and re-check the roster against the conflicts register before any rule is trusted.

🚪

The wall "leaks" via a side door

A Matter B document reaches a Matter A fee-earner through a shared mailbox or the DMS's own sharing. Be honest: the network wall governs reachability to the workspace segment; DMS-internal permissions and mail flow are the DMS's and mail platform's job. Record it as a finding for those controls — never claim the network rule should have caught it.

🧱

Cross-wall attempt not blocked

Rules are first-match top-down: check nothing above the wall rows (a broad site-to-site allow is the classic) matches first, that both directions are covered by the rule pair, and that the event attributes the flow to the user — an unattributed flow means the identity chain, not the wall, failed.

🕳️

The block happened but no event shows it

Tracking is per rule — a rule with Events unticked matches silently, and the blocked-attempt artefact is the criterion. Confirm every pilot row generates Events, then re-run the attempt.

⏱️

Team move slower than expected

Entra ID's automatic cycle runs roughly every 40 minutes, and on-demand provisioning does not assign group membership — wait for the cycle rather than "fixing" membership in Cato, where an IdP-sourced change would be overwritten anyway. Record the real window; it is the criterion.

🔍

DLP slice sees nothing

Almost always TLS inspection: the Legal classifiers need content, so the pilot cohort must be in TLSi scope with personal cloud storage not sitting in a bypass rule. Re-test with the fictional document after checking the TLSi events for the destination.

Gotchas & exit

Say these out loud before week one
  • The wall evidences separation; the firm's policy governs it. Conflicts decisions, wall declarations and the judgment that combined measures meet the SRA's "effective measures" bar remain the firm's and its COLP's — nothing here is legal advice, and the PoV output is input to that judgment, not a certificate.
  • Network reachability is the scope. DMS-internal permissions, mail, seating and undertakings are other layers of the barrier — this pilot proves the network layer, deliberately and only.
  • The monitor week is an Allow. For week one the cross-wall rows watch rather than block; the customer accepts that knowingly, and it is what makes the week-two block evidence honest.
  • The DLP slice is class-aware, not matter-aware — it evidences privileged-class material leaving, not one matter's files specifically. Keep the claim exactly that size.
  • The sync window is measured, not promised. Quote the timestamps from the drill, never "same day" as an adjective.

Unwinding cleanly: disable the four wall rows and the DLP slice rather than deleting them — hit counts and events are the pilot's record; de-scope the pilot groups from SCIM only if they were created for the PoV; and hand the evidence pack to the COLP whatever the verdict, because the questionnaire it answers will arrive regardless. If the decision is a yes, the pilot rows are the firm's first production wall — already live, already evidenced, and the pattern every subsequent conflict repeats.
