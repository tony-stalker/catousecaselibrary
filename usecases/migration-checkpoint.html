Check Point (Quantum & Harmony) to Cato

A Check Point estate concentrates pressure in two calendars that have now both passed — the December 2025 appliance end-of-support wave and R81.10's March 2026 end of support — and in a management-heavy operating model: SmartConsole layers, per-gateway blades, identity collectors and, since the Perimeter 81 acquisition, a second policy plane in Harmony SASE. This page maps Quantum, SmartConsole and Harmony to the Cato platform and lays out a phased migration in which every cutover is a route announcement — and every rollback a withdrawal.

Business objective

Why this matters — and why now

Nobody replaces a working Quantum estate on a whim. What puts this migration on the table is that Check Point's own lifecycle calendar is forcing spend on the status quo. A large cohort of widely deployed appliances — the 3100/3200, 5100–5900, 15400/15600 and 23500/23800/23900 series — reached end of support in December 2025 (the 5800 in September 2025), so any still in service is unsupported and a Quantum Force refresh quote is likely already on the table. Estates still on R81.10 have been out of support since March 2026 — the way out is a major-version upgrade project that must touch the management servers first, then every gateway. Either way, money and change windows are being spent; the honest question is whether to spend them renewing the appliance model or retiring it.

The second decision point is the operating model itself. Security value is sold as software blades enabled per gateway; policy lives in SmartConsole's ordered and inline layers; identity needs its own collector servers; and since the Perimeter 81 acquisition, Check Point's SASE answer — Harmony SASE — runs in its own portal application with its own agent and its own policy model, separate from the Quantum rulebase. Customers of the previous cloud service, Harmony Connect, have already been through one forced platform migration inside the same vendor (sk184452). The objective: retire the appliance and management estate on the customer's timetable, co-existing cleanly with Check Point throughout, with per-prefix rollback designed into every step.

Check Point's published support life cycle put a broad set of branch and DC appliances out of support in December 2025. The Quantum Force refresh quote that follows is exactly the moment a SASE alternative can be tabled — the spend is happening either way.

The software deadline R81.10 · Mar 2026

R80.40 went end of support in April 2024, R81 in October 2024, and R81.10 followed in March 2026. Staying current means a major-version project across the Security Management Server (first) and every gateway — recurring engineering effort that a cloud-delivered service simply removes.

One forced migration already sk184452

Harmony Connect — Check Point's first-generation cloud SSE — went end of sale at the end of 2023 with a formal EOL and service-termination notice. Its replacement, Harmony SASE, is the acquired Perimeter 81 stack (~$490m, completed September 2023): its own Infinity Portal application, agent and policy model, distinct from the Quantum rulebase. The "single platform" is in practice at least two policy planes.

The appliance is attack surface CVE-2024-24919

A zero-day information disclosure in internet-exposed Quantum gateways with Remote Access VPN or Mobile Access enabled was exploited in the wild from April 2024 and added to CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue. Hotfixes were required across R80.40–R81.20, Maestro, Scalable Chassis and Spark. On Cato, the PoPs are patched by Cato — there is no customer-side gateway hotfix cycle.

The Cato solution

Recommended migration path

Read this first

This page is authored from sourced research and the Cato Professional Services reference material — it is not a delivery-verified runbook. Review the plan with Cato Professional Services before using it with a customer.

The plan follows the Cato PS methodology — Discover & Design → Pilot, Build & Initial Rollout → Advanced Security & Broader Rollout → Tune, Optimise & Handover, across a typical four-month reference plan. Its heart is the parallel-socket pattern: the socket is installed alongside the Check Point edge, the Quantum gateway keeps the default gateway role, and a transit-VLAN handoff — static routes or eBGP (Gaia supports BGP natively) — moves subnets to Cato one prefix at a time. Cato-side routes are tagged with the Cato ASN and community 32768 so they can be isolated and filtered cleanly; for multi-site estates, an interconnecting hub site keeps legacy and Cato islands talking throughout.

Parallel build: the socket sits alongside the Quantum gateway, which keeps the default gateway role. The transit-VLAN handoff moves subnets to Cato one prefix at a time while user cohorts swap from Endpoint Security VPN (or the Harmony SASE agent) to the Cato Client in waves. Rollback at every step is a route withdrawal, not a rebuild.

  1. Export the estate and clean the rulebase

    Pull each Access Control layer with mgmt_cli show access-rulebase (or the Web API) using use-object-dictionary true, show-hits true and --format json. Two API realities to plan for: limit caps at 500 per call (paginate with offset), and each query returns a single layer — inline layers must be queried separately. The open-source ExportImportPolicyPackage tool exports a full policy package (R80.10+) for offline analysis. Capture NAT rules, Threat Prevention profiles, HTTPS Inspection policy, VPN communities, Identity Awareness sources and any Harmony SASE tenant configuration; on MDS, repeat per domain. Then clean before translating: use hit counts and last-modified metadata to strip zero-hit, disabled and shadowed rules and collapse duplicate objects — a decade of SmartConsole history translated verbatim just imports the mess.

  2. Build the parallel network

    Network → Sites

    Stand up sockets (or vSockets) alongside the Check Point estate with the Quantum gateway — or Maestro Security Group — still the L3 default gateway. Establish the transit-VLAN handoff per site: static routes or eBGP between socket and gateway, with Cato-side routes isolated by the Cato ASN and community 32768. Below roughly 20 sites, parallel connectivity at every site is the preferred pattern; at scale, add an interconnecting hub site (legacy CPE + socket) so legacy and Cato islands keep talking. Validate routing symmetry and return paths with test prefixes before any production move. Foundation work lands here too: CMA baseline, IdP/SCIM integration, SIEM export, and early distribution of the Cato Client and TLS root certificate.

  3. Swap user cohorts — SDP Migration Groups

    Access → Device Posture

    Run remote access as phased waves (the PS reference plan uses SDP Migration Groups 1–4 across months 2–4). Per cohort: enrol users via SCIM → enable the Cato Client → confirm application access → remove the legacy client — Endpoint Security VPN or the Harmony SASE agent — from that cohort's devices. Keep the swap short and scripted: third-party VPN drivers on the same machine can conflict with the Cato Client. Start Client Connectivity and Device Posture policy in monitor mode for device discovery, and tune between waves.

  4. Migrate sites in waves, one prefix at a time

    Security → TLS Inspection

    Move branches in groups; at each site's window, shift its subnets to the socket via the static or eBGP handoff rather than a big-bang LAN cutover — every prefix is individually revertible by withdrawing the route. Re-home anything DNAT-published through the Check Point NAT rulebase to Remote Port Forwarding (Cato allocated IPs) or Local Port Forwarding, update external DNS, and validate the return path before the gateway is retired. In parallel, rebuild HTTPS Inspection as phased Cato TLSi — block QUIC, certificate deployed, then gradual enablement from high-risk/low-impact categories outward — and bring IPS/NGAM through monitor-then-block, with CASB discovery and DLP in monitor mode once the baseline is stable.

  5. Decommission — management goes last

    Retire in dependency order: the legacy remote-access surface (Remote Access VPN / Mobile Access blades, Harmony SASE tenant) once the final cohort has migrated → branch gateways → DC gateways, Maestro Security Groups and orchestrators once traffic and events verify clean → Identity Collector servers → the Security Management Server / MDS last, only after SmartLog/SmartEvent data is exported or archived to satisfy retention obligations. Terminate blade subscriptions and support contracts at renewal.

Co-existence & rollback

Running both networks without breaking either

Placement is decided per site class in discovery: an L3 core switch can own the routing decision (keeping interim throughput off the Quantum gateway), the Check Point can stay as default gateway with the transit-VLAN handoff (the conservative default shown above), or the socket becomes default gateway with Alt-WAN / LAN BGP routing not-yet-migrated traffic back to the legacy network. Whichever pattern is chosen, every phase keeps a designed rollback lever:

PhaseRollback lever
Pilot siteRemove the static route or withdraw the BGP prefix at the switch or gateway — traffic reverts to Check Point instantly
Subnet migration (routed-range pattern)Re-add the dummy range on the Cato site and restore the statics at the interconnect — the designed rollback
BGP handoffWithdraw the prefix; routes propagate back automatically
User cohortsRe-enable the legacy client for the affected cohort — keep the VPN blades / Harmony SASE tenant warm until the final wave completes
TLS inspectionRoll back to an earlier enablement phase without disabling TLSi entirely
Full sitePhysical LAN cutover reversal — big-bang cutovers rate as “complex rollback”, which is why the parallel patterns are preferred
The big one: asymmetric routing through a stateful Check Point

If traffic egresses via Cato but returns via the Quantum gateway — or crosses regions through different hubs — Check Point's stateful inspection drops the unseen flow. ClusterXL and Maestro flow ownership make it strict about this. Design it out with parallel connectivity per region, hub anchoring, or AS-path design that keeps regional symmetry — and validate return paths before every cutover.

Double TLS inspection

If the HTTPS Inspection blade stays enabled on a path Cato now also inspects, users hit certificate-on-certificate failures and pinned apps break twice over. Disable gateway HTTPS Inspection for Cato-routed traffic, or scope Cato TLSi to bypass those paths until cutover.

Dual tunnel clients

Cato documents that third-party VPN drivers on the same machine can conflict with and override Cato Client settings. Keep the per-cohort swap short and scripted — install Cato Client → validate → uninstall Endpoint Security VPN or the Harmony SASE agent — and never leave two tunnel clients active long-term.

Inbound published services

Anything DNAT-published through the Check Point NAT rulebase must be consciously re-homed to Remote Port Forwarding (Cato allocated IPs) or Local Port Forwarding — with external DNS updated and the return route validated — before the gateway is retired. These move on their own change windows.

Identity continuity

Rules that depend on Identity Awareness (PDP/PEP-shared identities) need their Cato-side equivalents proven — SCIM/LDAP sync populated, Identity Agent deployed where non-Client users must be identified — before any gateway-side identity source is switched off.

Gotchas & objections

What will come up in the room

Layer semantics do not translate mechanically

In Check Point, an Accept in one Ordered Layer continues matching into the next (every layer must pass), each layer carries its own implicit cleanup action, Inline Layers nest parent/sub rules, and layers can be shared and delegated across policy packages. Cato's WAN/Internet/LAN firewalls are flat, first-match rulebases with fixed default postures. Model the effective policy — what actually passes all layers — not the layer structure, and budget conversion effort by layers and inter-layer dependencies, not raw rule count.

NAT lives in its own rulebase

Access policy and NAT policy are separate in SmartConsole, with automatic (object-driven) and manual NAT rules evaluated first-match. Inventory both types during export. Most branch hide-NAT simply disappears behind the PoP; DC manual NAT — static NAT, port translation, no-NAT exemptions between internal networks — needs line-by-line disposition against Cato's site-level NAT and RPF/LPF model.

Identity Awareness has infrastructure of its own

AD Query needs privileged WMI access to domain controllers; Identity Collector runs on Windows servers polling identity sources; PDP gateways share identities with PEP enforcement points. These are components to inventory and decommission — and rules that silently depend on identity (including in HTTPS Inspection and Application Control layers) must be flagged in discovery. Verify Cato licence coverage for every user the policy must identify.

“We need Maestro-class hyperscale”

Maestro load-shares up to 52 appliances behind orchestrators as one logical gateway — it solves an appliance scaling problem Cato does not have, because inspection capacity lives in the PoPs. The honest counter: DC egress/WAN throughput becomes a socket/vSocket sizing and HA design conversation, and the Maestro-specific operational surface — MHO firmware, SGM membership, Security Group design, dual-site sync — disappears entirely. Size formally; never quote throughput figures from memory.

VSX estates need a target tenancy model

Each Virtual System has its own interfaces, routing and policy. A single Cato account with scoped WAN FW / LAN FW rules usually replaces context separation; genuine multi-tenant requirements (divested units, regulated separation) may justify separate accounts. The hard case is overlapping IP space across Virtual Systems — surface it in week one, because it forces re-addressing or NAT design either way.

“Check Point has the best threat prevention”

Efficacy debates rarely land — anchor on operational reality instead. Blade-based protections are bounded by the appliance's sizing and software train; HTTPS Inspection is commonly constrained or switched off on appliances for performance and complexity reasons (Check Point maintains a dedicated best-practice SK for it); and CVE-2024-24919 showed the gateway itself is attack surface. Cato delivers IPS, NGAM, sandboxing and TLSi at every PoP, always current, with no customer patch cycle.

“We already own Harmony SASE”

It is a separate stack — the Perimeter 81-derived cloud, with its own agent and portal application, distinct from the Quantum rulebase. The estate still runs two policy planes and two inspection paths, and Harmony Connect customers have already been through one forced platform migration inside the same vendor (sk184452). Argue consoles-and-policies, not feature checklists.

Use the timing

The December 2025 hardware EOS wave and the R81.10 March 2026 end of support have both passed, so every estate still on those boxes or that train is already forced to spend on the status quo. Align the business case and pilot to land before the customer signs a Quantum Force refresh or an R81.20/R82 upgrade project — after that, the window closes for years.

PoV runbook

How to prove it in the customer's tenant

The displacement PoV is deliberately small: one branch, one parallel socket, the Quantum estate untouched. The only Check Point-side change in the whole engagement is a single route on the transit VLAN — SmartConsole, the management server, the blades and any Harmony SASE tenant all stay exactly as found, which is also what makes rollback a route withdrawal rather than a project. Everything else happens in the customer's Cato tenant: a bounded, hit-count-rationalised slice of the access layers translated monitor-first, HTTPS-inspection scope compared honestly through staged Cato TLSi, a pilot cohort swapped from the legacy client — the Harmony SASE agent where it is in the estate, Endpoint Security VPN otherwise — to the Cato Client, and threat prevention evidenced on real traffic. Prerequisites — agree these before week one
  • Trial licences confirmed for every capability under test — Cato Client (SDP) seats for the pilot cohort, IPS, Anti-Malware/NG Anti-Malware and TLS inspection — live for the whole PoV window.
  • TLS inspection staged first where content-level verdicts matter (Anti-Malware file verdicts, full-path URL matching) — follow the phased approach in TLS Inspection: Staged Rollout Playbook, with the Cato root certificate deployed to the pilot cohort only.
  • Identity in place — the pilot cohort exists as a SCIM/LDAP-provisioned group in CMA and is syncing before any user-scoped rule is written. The Check Point Identity Awareness estate stays untouched; Cato identity comes from the directory and the Client.
  • The SmartConsole baseline — a read-only mgmt_cli export of the branch's access layers with hit counts. This is the yardstick criteria 2 and 3 are measured against, and the only thing the PoV ever asks of the Check Point estate besides the handoff route.
  • Scope — one branch socket (shipped and staged), a transit VLAN agreed with the network team, and change windows for the handoff route and the rollback rehearsal.

  1. Week zero — capture the SmartConsole baseline before touching anything

    Export the pilot branch's access layers with hit counts and pick the representative slice: the 15–20 highest-hit rules as effective policy — what actually passes every ordered and inline layer, per the number-one gotcha above — deliberately including at least one identity-scoped rule and one URL Filtering / App Control rule, the two shapes that stress the translation. Also export the HTTPS Inspection rulebase and its bypass list: that is the honesty baseline for criterion 3. Dead rules stay dead — zero-hit rules are out of PoV scope by design, exactly as Check Point Policy Migration prescribes.

  2. Stand up the parallel socket at the pilot branch

    Network → Sites

    Create the site, connect the socket on the transit VLAN and let it tunnel out through the existing internet path — the Quantum gateway remains L3 default gateway and nothing changes for users. Confirm in Monitor → Topology that the site is up against its nearest PoP, and take the dated day-one screenshots: this is evidence item zero for criterion 1.

  3. Configure the handoff — statics or eBGP to Gaia

    Network → Sites → Site Settings → BGP

    Per the co-existence pattern above, either point statics for the pilot subnet at the socket from the L3 switch or gateway, or bring up an eBGP neighbour to Gaia (which supports BGP natively): set the peer and Cato ASNs in ASN Settings, the peer IP, and advertise Summary routes towards the gateway with the Metric set so the Quantum still prefers its own default; verify with Show BGP Status (Configuring BGP Neighbors for a Cato Socket). Keep learned legacy routes isolated from the rest of the Cato account by tagging them with the Cato ASN and community 32768 (socket v15+ — see Migration Methodology). Either way, the pilot subnet now reaches Cato per prefix — and the rollback lever for criterion 6 already exists.

  4. Translate the access-layer slice — monitor-first

    Security → WAN Firewall Security → Internet Firewall

    Flatten the slice into the two rulebases: east-west intent into the WAN Firewall allowlist, egress intent into the Internet Firewall — and wherever an inline layer's implicit cleanup did the dropping, write the explicit block rule that layer never had to spell out. Every translated rule gets Event tracking; nothing blocks that the Quantum did not already block. The full four-domain method, layer-semantics traps and a worked before/after example live on Check Point Policy Migration — the PoV runs that method on the slice only.

  5. Stage TLS inspection — and make the comparison honest

    Security → TLS Inspection

    Enabling TLSi inspects all HTTPS by default, so scope it deliberately: run the wizard to lay down the bypass-first rules (Bypass Sensitive Categories, Bypass Embedded Operating Systems) and start with the pilot cohort as the test group, per Cato's controlled-rollout guidance (TLS Inspection Configuration Wizard · Best Practices for TLS Inspection). Deploy the root certificate to the cohort, block QUIC so web traffic stays inspectable, and carry the intent of the blade's bypass list — never its rules. Then put the two rulebases side by side: on most estates the HTTPS Inspection blade runs constrained or off for performance reasons, so expect Cato to decrypt more than the blade ever did — say so up front, and count the new detections as findings, not regressions.

  6. Swap the pilot cohort to the Cato Client

    Access → Client Connectivity Policy Access → Users

    Confirm the SCIM/LDAP group is populated, then switch each machine scripted and short: install Cato Client → validate application access → uninstall the legacy client — the Harmony SASE agent where the customer runs it, Endpoint Security VPN otherwise. Do not leave both resident: third-party VPN drivers on the same machine can conflict with the Cato Client (see co-existence above). Keep the VPN blades and any Harmony tenant warm — they are the rollback lever for criterion 4, not day-one waste.

  7. Threat prevention — monitor, then block, on the pilot scope

    Security → IPS Security → Anti-Malware

    Enable IPS in Monitor — verdicts generate events while traffic continues (Configuring the IPS Policy) — and Anti-Malware with NG Anti-Malware on top of it (it requires the base engine enabled; Configuring the Anti-Malware Policy). Note for the comparison with SandBlast: encrypted-file and full-content verdicts depend on the TLSi scope staged in step 5. Review a week of verdicts with the customer, then flip the pilot scope to block and demonstrate a harmless test detection stopped, block page shown, event attributed to user, device and site — criterion 5 evidenced in one screen.

  8. Build the evidence pack — and rehearse the rollback

    Monitor → Events

    Filter events per translated rule and set the counts against the week-zero show-hits baseline: same flows, same intent, now with user and device attribution — and generate the rule hit count report from Home → Reports as the dated PDF artefact (rule hit count reports). Capture the cohort's Experience Monitoring week and the threat events. Then close the loop on criterion 6 in a change window: withdraw the handoff route (remove the static or disable the BGP neighbour), watch traffic revert to the Quantum gateway, time it, screenshot it, restore it. A rollback that has been rehearsed and measured is the strongest slide in the wrap-up deck.

What good output looks like

Troubleshooting the PoV

🔀

Asymmetric routing through the Quantum

Migrated-subnet sessions stall or reset: traffic egressed via Cato but returned via the stateful gateway, whose inspection drops the unseen flow — and ClusterXL flow ownership makes it strict about this. Check SmartLog for out-of-state drops to the pilot subnet; fix by advertising the pilot prefix consistently in both directions and never splitting a flow's egress and return between the two edges.

🧩

A flow passes on Cato that the blade dropped

Almost always an inline layer's implicit cleanup that never became an explicit rule — configuration nobody wrote, silently lost in flattening. Re-derive the effective policy for that flow across every layer and add the missing block with the parent rule's scope. This is the policy page's number-one gotcha showing up on live traffic.

🔐

Double TLS inspection

Pinned apps failing, certificate warnings doubling up: the HTTPS Inspection blade is still active on a path Cato now also inspects. Disable blade inspection for Cato-routed pilot traffic, keep the wizard's bypass rules ahead of every inspect rule, and confirm QUIC is blocked so browsers cannot sidestep the inspected path.

🪪

Identity-scoped rules that never match

A translated access-role rule shows zero events: the SCIM/LDAP group is empty or stale in CMA, so the rule cannot match. Remember Cato identity comes from the directory and the Client — not from AD Query or Identity Collector, which keep serving the Check Point side untouched. Check group membership under Access → Users before touching the rule.

📴

Cohort machine sends no traffic via Cato

The legacy client was left installed alongside the Cato Client, and its driver is overriding Client settings — the documented dual-VPN conflict, and Harmony SASE's agent counts. Uninstall it, reboot, reconnect. Keep the per-machine swap scripted so the two clients never cohabit longer than validation takes.

📈

New detections read as regressions

Events appear on Cato with no SmartConsole ancestor: usually traffic the blade never decrypted, now inspected for the first time under staged TLSi. Triage each against the week-zero HTTPS Inspection export — if the blade bypassed or never saw the flow, it is a finding for the wrap-up, not a parity failure. Compare on flow outcomes, never on rule names.

Gotchas & exit

Boundaries to state up front
  • Each capability under test needs its trial licence live for the whole PoV window — a licence lapsing mid-pilot reads as a product failure.
  • TLS inspection is automatically bypassed for Android, Linux and unidentified operating systems — keep those devices out of the pilot cohort or scope criterion 3 around them.
  • Maestro sizing, VSX tenancy design and DC east-west policy are out of PoV scope by design — they are migration-phase workstreams; park them in the next-steps register, per the framework page.
  • Export the evidence pack weekly, not at the end — screenshots and event exports dated as they land, so the wrap-up reads a register rather than excavating history.

Exit has two doors, both clean. If the criteria pass, nothing is thrown away: the pilot branch, transit-VLAN handoff and cohort become the first wave of the migration path above, and the parked asks become the rollout scope. If the customer stops, the unwind is the rollback rehearsal run once more without the restore: withdraw the handoff route, re-enable the legacy client for the cohort (the VPN blades and Harmony tenant stayed warm throughout), uninstall the Cato Client, and export the evidence pack and Administration → Audit Trail before trial licences lapse. The Check Point estate ends the engagement exactly as it started — one route removed, nothing else ever changed.
