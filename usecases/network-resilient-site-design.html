Resilient Site Design

Every site fails eventually — a digger through the fibre, a dead power supply, a PoP taken out of rotation. Resilience isn't a default you inherit; it's a design decision made per site class, and a kiosk shouldn't get the same design (or the same spend) as a datacentre. This page sets out the reference patterns from small branch to critical DC, the Cato building blocks behind them — dual last miles, LTE last resort, HA Socket pairs, PoP failover and off-cloud recovery — and the runbook to prove they actually work.

Business objective

Design against the failure, not the brochure

Most availability conversations start with an SLA percentage and end with an invoice. A better conversation starts with the question: what exactly can fail at this site, and what happens to the people in the building when it does? On Cato there are four distinct failure domains, and each has its own building block — so you can match resilience (and cost) to how much each site actually matters.

1 · Last-mile loss

The everyday failure: a cut circuit, a provider maintenance window, a degraded DSL line. Answered by a second last mile with SLA-driven steering — and LTE/5G as the final backstop.

2 · Socket hardware loss

A failed PSU or a bricked device. Answered by an HA Socket pair on sites that can't wait for a hardware swap — and a cold-spare process everywhere else.

3 · PoP unreachable or degraded

Rarer: trouble between the ISP and the Cato PoP, or a PoP out of rotation. Answered automatically — the Socket evaluates its links and moves to another PoP, with preferred primary and secondary locations if you want to steer it.

4 · Total off-cloud

The site is powered, the LAN is up, but no PoP is reachable. Answered by recovery modes: internet traffic breaks out locally, WAN traffic re-forms over Socket-to-Socket DTLS tunnels, and the local LAN firewall keeps east-west policy running.

Per-second SLA steering

The Socket scores every link every second on loss, jitter and latency, and distributes flows by bandwidth ratio. A degrading link sheds traffic before users open tickets.

👥

HA without drama

Standby takes over after 3 s of missed keepalives, announcing itself with GARP for fast Layer-2 convergence — and the Cato Cloud maintains flow state through the transition.

📶

LTE that doesn't surprise finance

Last-resort links carry only minimal Cato system traffic while idle, precisely to keep cellular data cost near zero until the day you need it.

🌐

Graceful degradation off-cloud

Internet Recovery, Socket-to-Socket WAN Recovery tunnels and the on-Socket LAN firewall mean “no PoP reachable” degrades the site — it doesn't kill it.

Dual last miles — active/active with link precedence

WAN links take a precedence role: 1 (active), 2 (passive) or 3 (last resort). Active/active distribution is flow-based (5-tuple), so a conversation sticks to one link; flows are shared in proportion to configured bandwidth — a 100 Mbps and a 20 Mbps link get flows at roughly 5:1. Every second, each link is scored against health thresholds (packet loss 3%, jitter 30 ms, latency 600 ms); links that miss them are deprioritised, and a dead link's flows move to the surviving link immediately. The Socket steers upstream; the PoP steers downstream — so both directions are managed. Passive links activate when all active links are down or fail the Connection SLA, and stand down after 10 minutes of restored health on the higher-precedence links.

LTE / 5G as last resort — and its caveats

A last-resort (precedence 3) link — typically cellular, via the X1600 LTE Socket or an external modem — activates only when all active and passive links are down or fail the SLA thresholds, and deactivates once a precedence 1 or 2 link has met the SLA for a continuous 10 minutes. Design caveats from the documentation worth repeating in the room:

  • While idle it carries only minimal Cato system traffic — good for cost, but it means the cellular path is not continuously proven under load. Test it.
  • Off-cloud (Socket-to-Socket) traffic is not supported over last-resort links, and off-cloud transport is automatically disabled on precedence-3 links.
  • An optional grace timer (default 0 s) delays activation to ride out blips — but any value above 0 means the site is dark during the wait.
  • Recommended configuration: exclude the link from last-mile monitoring and relax its keepalive settings, so monitoring itself doesn't burn the data plan.

HA Socket pairs — requirements and readiness

Socket HA is an active/standby pair of the same model family (X1500 pairs with X1500B; an X1600 cannot pair with an X1600 LTE), supported for physical sites and AWS/Azure vSockets. Cabling must be symmetrical — both Sockets mirrored into the same LAN and the same ISPs — with the secondary's management IP inside the site's native range. The pair exchanges VRRP keepalives over a designated LAN port (default VRID 100). Failover triggers: 3 seconds of missed keepalives, primary hardware/power failure, or the master losing all internet connectivity for more than 10 seconds; the standby announces itself with GARP, and an HA event is raised if the secondary stays active beyond 35 seconds. The CMA rolls health into one word — Ready — built from three indicators:

IndicatorGreen means
ConnectedBoth Sockets hold their own working tunnel to the Cato Cloud
KeepaliveExactly one primary and one secondary are talking — this is also the split-brain check
Compatible VersionBoth Sockets run the same major Socket OS version
ReadyAll three OK — the site can fail over cleanly. Anything else is a ticket, not trivia.

PoP selection and failover

By default a connectivity algorithm picks the best PoP — the recommendation is to leave it on Automatic and set the site's city. For critical sites you can define preferred primary and secondary PoP locations, and optionally restrict the site to them. If every link fails the SLA against the current PoP, the Socket connects to a different PoP and runs an initial SLA evaluation of 40–50 seconds — returning to the original if nothing better is found. A Socket that lands on a non-preferred PoP automatically moves back after 60 minutes by default (tunable or disabled).

Off-cloud: what still works when no PoP is reachable

The Socket is not a thin client — it degrades deliberately when the Cato Cloud is unreachable:

  • Internet Recovery (enabled by default) — on tunnel loss (100% packet loss, or three consecutive missed keepalives) the Socket designates a link and sends internet traffic straight out via the local ISP from its own public IP. Reconnection uses a back-off (8 s doubling up to 1024 s) to prevent flapping, and traffic returns to Cato the moment keepalives resume.
  • WAN Recovery — site-to-site WAN traffic re-forms over encrypted off-cloud DTLS tunnels between Sockets, full mesh by default. Estates with hundreds of sites can use a hub-and-spoke off-cloud topology instead — spokes tunnel to hub sites only, which trades spoke-to-spoke recovery for far less tunnel/keepalive load on small Sockets. Off-cloud traffic doesn't count against the site's bandwidth licence; sites need unique public IPs, and a Socket supports at most three off-cloud links.
  • Alt-WAN Recovery (limited release) — sites keeping an MPLS/alt-WAN underlay can automatically reroute via it to another Socket site, which forwards traffic on to the Cato Cloud. Not compatible with BGP-connected sites.
  • The LAN keeps its firewall — the Socket Next Gen LAN Firewall enforces Layer 2–7 east-west policy on the Socket itself and routes matching traffic locally, so segmentation, OT and IoT keep operating even with the internet down entirely.
  • IPsec and cloud-interconnect sites get none of this — no Internet or WAN Recovery. Their resilience is tunnel design: primary and secondary tunnels to two different PoPs.
Recovery is a degraded mode — treat it like one

Traffic in Internet, WAN or Alt-WAN Recovery bypasses Cato's cloud inspection — firewall, IPS and threat protection are not applied, and off-cloud transport is likewise uninspected. Recommended practice: decide per site class whether that trade is acceptable (a PCI estate may prefer to disable Internet Recovery and accept the outage), and alert on recovery events so nobody quietly runs uninspected for a week.

BGP for the sites that route

Datacentres and MPLS-coexistence sites should speak BGP to the Cato Cloud over the LAN or alt-WAN interface — no tunnels required — so routes are learned dynamically instead of maintained by hand, inheriting the site's security policy. The resilience property that matters: when a Socket disconnects, its site's ranges are withdrawn, so traffic immediately follows the next-best BGP path — the standard active/standby DC pattern. BFD can be configured for BGP neighbours to speed failure detection. Two limits to design around: no graceful restart for route withdrawal, and 1024 received prefixes per neighbour by default.

Design review & demo runbook

Run the design review like an incident that hasn't happened yet

Testing discipline

Resilience you haven't tested is a diagram, not a design. Every pattern on this page ends the same way: pull the link, in a change window, on purpose — and watch the platform do what the slide claimed.

  1. Tier the estate first

    Agree three or four site classes with the customer and an honest recovery objective for each — “the kiosk can be down for a day; the DC can't be down for a minute”. Every design choice below hangs off this, and it's recommended practice, not product configuration.

  2. Walk the link design of a real site

    Network → Sites

    Open a branch site's Socket configuration: show each WAN port's precedence (1 active, 2 passive, 3 last resort), bandwidth settings, and the per-link off-cloud toggle. This is where the site-class pattern becomes visible configuration.

  3. Show the SLA and last-resort safety rails

    Network → Sites

    Review the Connection SLA thresholds (account-level or per-site), the last-resort grace timer, and the LTE link's exclusion from last-mile monitoring. Explain the 10-minute stand-down: backup links don't flap back the moment the primary blinks healthy.

  4. Verify HA readiness on the critical sites

    Monitor → Topology

    Open an HA site and walk the three indicators behind Ready — Connected, Keepalive, Compatible Version. Anything not green is a standing action. For maintenance windows, note that failover can also be activated manually from the CMA.

  5. Confirm the PoP strategy

    Network → Sites

    Automatic PoP selection with the city set for standard sites; preferred primary and secondary PoPs for critical ones. Mention the 60-minute automatic return to the preferred PoP so nobody is surprised by a planned reconnect.

  6. Pull a link in a change window

    Monitor → Topology

    The proof. Disconnect the primary WAN at a pilot site and watch the link go down and flows re-home to the surviving link in Topology; then restore it and watch the platform settle back. On an HA site, fail the master Socket once too.

  7. Close on the audit trail

    Monitor → Events

    Filter events for the test site: link down, SLA violations, HA failover, recovery mode. This is the evidence pack for the change record — and the alerting feed that should page someone when a real failover happens. Re-run the test after ISP changes or Socket upgrades.

PoV runbook

How to prove it in the customer's tenant

This PoV is a resilience drill card: every failure mode in the design above becomes a timed, witnessed drill on one pilot site, run in a change window, with the expected behaviour written down from the knowledge base before the drill and the measured result stamped on the card after it. Run it inside the frame from Running a Cato PoV — criteria agreed at the scope workshop, evidence dated as it lands. The SD-WAN PoV already proves a single link pull with voice continuity; this runbook goes further — LTE last resort, Socket HA, PoP behaviour and power-loss recovery — and it deliberately doubles as a change-management test: if the customer's process can't book, execute and record five planned failures, that finding belongs in the wrap-up too.

Success criteria — the drill card

Agree the card at the workshop: which drills the pilot hardware supports, the recovery objective per drill (from the site-class tiering above), and who witnesses. Timing is measured by stopwatch and a continuous ping log; CMA events corroborate — they don't replace the watch, because event thresholds round up (see gotchas). Prerequisites — run the drills your hardware supports

The card is honest about fit: a single-link, single-Socket pilot site can only run D4 and D5 — mark the rest not applicable rather than pretending. Map before the workshop:

  • D1 needs two last miles at the pilot site — any commodity pair.
  • D2 needs LTE/5G fitted — an X1600 LTE Socket or an external modem, configured as precedence 3, with an agreed data budget for the drill minutes.
  • D3 needs an HA pair showing Ready; manual failover from the CMA requires both Sockets on Socket OS version 24 or higher, and someone physically on site during the drill.
  • D4 and D5 need any Socket site — for D4, set preferred primary and secondary PoPs on the critical tier first so there is a configuration to verify; D5 needs physical access to the power feed.
  • Every drill needs a booked change window and a change record — the HQ lab archetype is the ideal pilot because a drill that misbehaves inconveniences nobody.
  • Test harness: a softphone call for D1/D2, a continuous timestamped ping from a LAN endpoint for every drill, and a stopwatch (a phone video of the console clock works — it captures timing and witness in one artefact).
  • Not needed: TLS inspection or identity integration — this is a transport PoV, which keeps privacy sign-off off the critical path.

Configuration walkthrough

  1. Map the drill card to the pilot hardware

    Network → Sites

    Open the pilot site's Socket configuration and read off what it actually has: WAN port precedence roles (1 active, 2 passive, 3 last resort), whether LTE is fitted, whether the site is an HA pair. Strike unsupported drills from the card in writing at the workshop — the drills you run are determined by the hardware, not the slideware.

  2. Capture the steady state — the rollback target

    Monitor → Topology

    Before anything is pulled, capture the state every drill must return to: tunnels up on all links, link roles, current PoP, and on the HA site the three indicators behind Ready — Connected, Keepalive, Compatible Version (What is Socket HA). Every rollback column on the card means "matches this capture". An HA site that is not Ready does not get drill D3 until it is — that is a finding, not a blocker.

  3. Arm the evidence feed and learn its timing

    Home → Events

    Filter Connectivity events for the pilot site and keep the view open through every drill. Know the semantics before you rely on them: Connected fires after a tunnel has been up for 30 seconds; Disconnected only fires when an active (precedence 1) link's tunnel is down for more than 2½ minutes; a quick restore lands as Reconnected; last-resort and passive links raise Passive Connected/Passive Disconnected instead (connectivity events). This is why the stopwatch is the measurement and the event is the corroboration.

  4. Drill D1 — pull the primary link

    Network → Sites → Site Monitoring → Real Time

    Voice call up, ping running, cable out, watch the Transport tab as throughput shifts to the surviving link. Expected per the KB: packets belonging to flows on the dead link are sent to the link that is still up, with at most a slight, temporary disruption for connection-sensitive applications (Active/Active Traffic Distribution). The call should not drop. Restore the cable, watch flows redistribute, and stamp the card: measured gap in the ping log, call verdict, event IDs.

  5. Drill D2 — pull the second link onto LTE

    Network → Sites → Site Monitoring → Real Time

    With the primary still out, pull the remaining wired link. Expected: the last-resort link activates only now — when all active and passive links are down or failing SLA — after the grace timer, which defaults to 0 s (Configuring a Last-Resort Link). Be honest in the room about the caveats: the drill minutes carry full site traffic over cellular at cellular prices; off-cloud (Socket-to-Socket) traffic is not supported on a last-resort link; and rollback is slow by design — LTE stands down only after a wired link has met the SLA for 10 continuous minutes, so book the window accordingly.

  6. Drill D3 — fail over the HA pair

    Monitor → Topology

    Two variants. Controlled: from the Socket's Actions menu choose Activate Manual HA Failover (both Sockets on version 24+, state Ready) — the primary stops sending VRRP keepalives, the secondary takes over, and the CMA transiently shows both as primary with "HA not ready"; that display is documented as expected. Wait about 120 seconds and refresh to verify, then revert with Stop HA Failover (Manually Activate Socket HA Failover). Real: pull power on the primary — takeover on 3 s of missed keepalives, GARP announcement, Socket Fail-Over event once the secondary has been active 35 s. Heed the KB warning: manual failover is a test tool, not a maintenance method — using it to hold the pair failed over invites split-brain. Someone stays on site throughout.

  7. Drill D4 — verify the PoP failover posture

    Network → Sites

    The honest drill: PoP failover is automatic and the KB documents no supported way to force it on demand — reconnects happen during PoP maintenance or when every link fails the Connection SLA against the current PoP. So D4 verifies the posture rather than staging the failure: preferred primary and secondary PoP locations set on the critical tier, the restrict-to-preferred option a deliberate choice (restricted sites will not connect elsewhere even in trouble), and the 60-minute automatic return understood so a planned reconnect surprises nobody (Defining a Preferred PoP for a Site). If a Changed PoP event lands naturally during the pilot, capture it — that is the live proof; if not, the card records "configuration and event trail verified", and says so plainly.

  8. Drill D5 — kill the power

    Home → Events

    Last drill of the window: power off the Socket (on the HA site, the whole pair — the single-Socket takeover was D3), wait one minute, power on, and time from switch-on to the first successful ping from the LAN endpoint. No hands on keyboards — the point is that recovery is unattended. Time the Socket separately from the comms stack if the modem/ONT also lost power, so the minutes are attributed fairly. Close by confirming the site matches the steady-state capture from step 2: link roles, current PoP, HA Ready.

What good output looks like

By the wrap-up the drill card is fully stamped: every drill dated, timed, witnessed, with its event IDs and its return-to-steady-state confirmation — and every struck drill marked not applicable with the hardware reason. A failed drill is recorded with the same care as a pass, classified setup or product (see troubleshooting): "LTE never activated because a passive link was still healthy" is a design conversation, not a product defect, and saying so is what makes the rest of the card credible. The connectivity-event timeline from Home → Events is the spine of the evidence pack; the ping logs and stopwatch times hang off it.

Troubleshooting — setup failure or product failure?

The card's credibility rests on classifying every miss correctly. The first three are setup problems wearing a product costume; the last three are documented behaviour that only looks like failure if nobody read the manual:

📶

LTE never activated (setup)

Last resort fires only when all active and passive links are down or failing SLA. A forgotten precedence-2 link that is still healthy — or a grace timer somebody set above 0 — keeps LTE idle. Check the link roles and the timer before blaming the modem.

🚫

Manual HA failover refused (setup)

It requires both Sockets on version 24+ and the pair in Ready state. A red Keepalive or Compatible Version indicator is the actual finding — fix the cabling or the version skew, capture that as evidence, then drill.

🔌

Cold boot looked slow (setup)

If the modem/ONT lost power too, its boot time dominates the measurement. Time the Socket from the moment its uplink is genuinely available, or drill the Socket's power feed alone — attribute the minutes to the right box.

⏱

No Disconnected event after a pull (product — by design)

Disconnected fires only after an active link's tunnel is down for over 2½ minutes; a fast drill lands as Reconnected instead. The ping log and Transport graphs are the sub-minute evidence — that's why the stopwatch is the measurement.

🧠

Both Sockets showed primary (product — expected, briefly)

During a manual failover the transient both-primary, "HA not ready" display is documented behaviour confirming takeover. Sustained split-brain is different — usually keepalive cabling, or manual failover left active as a maintenance hack. Restart the primary to restore.

🐢

LTE wouldn't stand down (product — by design)

Deactivation needs a higher-precedence link healthy for 10 continuous minutes, and the timer resets on every wobble. A flapping restored link keeps the site on cellular legitimately — evidence for the "order diverse circuits" conversation, not a defect.

Gotchas & exit

Honest caveats to set at the workshop
  • Every drill is a deliberate outage. Never run one during business hours without change control — and treat the change process itself as under test. A customer whose process can't schedule five planned failures in four weeks has learned something important about their real recovery posture.
  • Event timestamps round up. The 30-second Connected dwell and the 2½-minute Disconnected threshold mean event times understate how fast the platform actually moved. Quote the stopwatch and ping log as the measurement; cite events as corroboration.
  • Don't promise the Failover event by name. The KB documents the WAN-link Failover sub-type for Socket v8.0 and earlier — on current Sockets, evidence a link pull with Transport graphs and the Connected/Reconnected pair instead.
  • LTE drills cost real money. Idle last-resort links carry only minimal system traffic, but drill minutes carry the site's full traffic over cellular. Agree the data budget before D2, not on the invoice.
  • PoP failover cannot be staged on demand. Set that expectation at the workshop so D4's "posture verified" outcome is the agreed pass, not a disappointment.
Unwinding cleanly at the end

There is little to unwind — the drills change nothing permanent by design. Confirm any manual HA failover was stopped and the pair shows Ready, revert any precedence or grace-timer values altered for testing, remove PoV-only notification recipients, and check the site against the steady-state capture one final time. The stamped drill card is the artefact that survives: it walks into the decision meeting as measured proof the designs on this page fail the way the slides claim — and it becomes the customer's own recurring test plan, re-run after every ISP change or Socket upgrade.
