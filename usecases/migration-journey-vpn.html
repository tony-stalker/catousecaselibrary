VPN to ZTNA: Animated Phased Journey

A VPN replacement moves people, not circuits — so it stalls the same way every WAN migration does: in the middle, with some users on the new client, some on the old, and contractors nobody wants to touch. This page is the animated companion to the full remote-access playbooks: press play and watch a concentrator estate at London DC retire one cohort at a time — private apps in the datacentre, more in Azure, full-tunnel backhaul giving way to always-on ZTNA with the headends switched off.

Business objective

Cohorts, not cutovers

Site migrations co-exist by routing; client migrations co-exist by cohort. At any moment mid-rollout some users run the legacy VPN agent and some run the Cato Client — but no machine runs both full-tunnel, and the headends stay warm as the rollback path until the last wave clears its soak window. The one piece of routing this journey does need — a Socket at London DC and a vSocket in Azure, putting both app estates on Cato before any cohort moves — goes in first. That is the pattern the animation below plays out. For the full playbooks — discovery, posture mapping, MDM mechanics — see AnyConnect to Cato Client and DirectAccess to Cato SDP; for why per-app ZTNA beats network-wide VPN access for third parties, see De-risking 3rd-Party Supply Chain Access.

The walkthrough

The VPN retirement, phase by phase

Three groups of users — a pilot cohort, the departmental waves behind them, and the contractors and BYOD devices sharing the same VPN — start out full-tunnelling to the VPN headends at London DC, where the private apps live; traffic for the apps in Azure hairpins through the same datacentre. The upper band is the Cato backbone. Watch the rail go in first — a Socket at London DC, a vSocket in Azure — then each cohort's lane swing up to the nearest PoP, access tighten from network-wide to per-app, and the headends drain to standby before the final switch-off. Autoplay steps every few seconds; use the dots or arrows to move at your own pace.

At each phase

What changes, and who connects how

PhaseWhat changesWho connects how
0 · Legacy Baseline — every employee and contractor full-tunnels to the headends at London DC, which must be sized for peak, patched on the vendor's schedule and left listening on the internet. Apps in Azure are reached by hairpinning through the same DC. Everyone on the VPN; one tunnel grants the whole network, and Azure-bound traffic rides the DC hairpin.
1 · Cato rail The app side goes on Cato before any user moves: a Socket at London DC, a vSocket in the Azure vNet, IdP SSO + MFA connected, posture profiles in monitor. Both app estates are now reachable over the backbone — and the Azure hairpin collapses to a one-hop Cato path. Everyone still on the live headends; only the apps have moved.
2 · Pilot cohort MDM swaps the pilot cohort — remove the VPN profile, install the Cato Client — in one change window. Pilot users get ZTNA to named apps in both locations; their VPN tunnel falls dormant as the rollback path. Pilot on ZTNA via the nearest PoP to apps at London DC and in Azure; everyone else still on the headends.
3 · Cohort waves Departmental waves repeat the pilot's swap and acceptance checklist, and contractors and BYOD move to clientless browser access — no agent on devices you don't manage, no network address issued. Network-wide grants give way to per-app least-privilege rules; the headends drain to standby, kept warm only as the rollback path. Everyone on Cato; headends idle through the final soak window.
4 · Headends off Tunnel-groups disabled, the concentrators at London DC decommissioned, support renewals and client licences cancelled. Posture flips from monitor to enforce and always-on protects users before they think about connecting. Always-on ZTNA to both app estates — and no internet-exposed headend left to patch.
Why nobody loses access mid-flight

Cohorts swap clients rather than stacking two full-tunnel agents on one machine, so each user is always on exactly one working path — and rollback is a per-cohort MDM change measured in minutes, cheap only for as long as the headends stay racked, licensed and monitored. Contractors never install anything at all, which is precisely why they move on the clientless wave. The mechanics — discovery, posture mapping, the helpdesk playbook — are in AnyConnect to Cato Client and DirectAccess to Cato SDP.
