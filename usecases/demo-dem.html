Demo Segment: Digital Experience Monitoring

Segment 7 of the 60-minute platform demo — minutes 40 to 45, the last five minutes of screen time. Every flow the previous six segments showed produced telemetry; this segment turns it into evidence. Close on a per-user, per-app experience score and the fault domain found hop by hop, so the question the prospect will hear most — “is it slow because of Cato?” — is answered from the console, not argued in a war room.

Why this segment

The one sentence they must leave with

Because the Client, the Socket, the PoP and the backbone are one platform, Cato sees every hop a user's traffic takes — and scores each one, so “the app is slow” resolves to a named segment in seconds.

Users do not experience a network or a security stack; they experience a Teams call that stutters or a CRM page that hangs. The ticket says “the app is slow”, and the truth sits somewhere along a path that crosses the laptop, the home Wi-Fi, the ISP, the middle mile and the application itself — each with a different owner and a different definition of “fine”. Traditional monitoring watches the infrastructure the organisation owns, which is exactly the part of a hybrid worker's path the traffic barely touches.

Experience Monitoring gives every site, user, host and application a score — Good, Fair or Poor — and decomposes it node by node in Connection Details: device hardware, Wi-Fi, LAN gateway, last mile, PoP, application. Each node is scored independently against published thresholds, and application scores are judged against an AI baseline built from real traffic for that application. This is the segment that lands best with a network or operations audience, which is why the cut plan says never to drop it for them.

The five-minute story

Bridge in, three beats, bridge out

Bridge in — from App Connectors

“Every one of those flows produces telemetry, and that telemetry answers the question you will hear most: is it slow because of Cato?”

  1. Beat 1 — the scored view across users and sites

    Say: “This is the helpdesk queue before it happens. Every site and every user carries an experience score, and sorting worst-first shows who is about to call.”

    Show: Home → Experience Monitoring — the summary bar, the Account Experience graph, the story feed, then the Sites and Remote Users tabs sorted by experience.

  2. Beat 2 — drill one user to the failing node

    Say: “Pick the user at the top. One screen decomposes their path — device, Wi-Fi, LAN gateway, last mile, PoP, application — and scores each piece on its own. The one amber node is the fault domain. Not the laptop, not Cato, not the app.”

    Show: the user drill-down, Connection Details node chain, then Path Analysis for the hop-by-hop route from the device to the PoP with per-hop loss and latency.

  3. Beat 3 — the proactive angle

    Say: “Passive scoring is the default. On top of it you define synthetic probes for the applications you care about, the anomaly engine raises stories when a site-and-app pair drifts from its baseline, and the Site Experience report lands on a schedule.”

    Show: Home → Experience Monitoring Probes for the probe policy, then the Site Experience template under Home → Reports.

Bridge out — to the close

“One console, seven capabilities, thirty-five minutes of screen time. What would you want to see proven on your own traffic?”

What the engine does

Same engine, seen from the telemetry side

The six earlier segments showed the single-pass engine making decisions: allow, inspect, block. This segment shows what the same engine observes while it does so. Because the Cato Client on the device, the Socket at the site, the PoP and the global private backbone are one platform, Cato measures every hop the traffic actually takes — and the decision this capability makes is a score per segment, so the failing one is measured rather than inferred.

User and site traffic through the engine, measured at every hop
The decision this capability makes is a score per segment. Loss at ② with ③ and ④ clean is the ISP's circuit; a slow ④ with ①–③ green is the application's side of the fence. Either way the verdict is measured, not negotiated.
🎯

Fault domain in one view

Device, Wi-Fi, LAN gateway, last mile, PoP and application scored side by side in Connection Details — the conversation starts at the failing node, not at “whose fault is it?”.

🧑‍💻

Per-user, per-app truth

Every sanctioned application a user touches carries its own Good / Fair / Poor score against that app's AI baseline, so “slow” resolves to which app, how much and since when.

📄

Evidence for ISP disputes

Overlay and underlay probes separate the circuit from the tunnel, and Path Analysis keeps hop-by-hop loss and latency historically — “no fault found” becomes a documented circuit case.

📈

Proactive, not reactive

Synthetic probes on your terms, anomaly stories when a site-and-app pair drifts from its 14-day baseline, and scheduled Site Experience reports — degradation surfaces before users call it in.

Demo runbook

Minutes 40 to 45, step by step

Five minutes of screen time. The prospect has just watched a private application published without inbound rules; this segment proves the platform can also tell them how that application, and every other one, actually feels to the user. Practically: confirm the demo tenant has a DEM licence before the call, and pre-select the user you will drill so the chain is already loaded.

  1. Open the scored view and sort worst-first

    Home → Experience Monitoring

    Say: “Account Experience is Good, so there is no global problem — anything degraded is local and findable. The feed on the right is the platform raising link-quality stories by itself, and the table is the helpdesk queue before it happens.”

    Show: the summary bar, the Account Experience graph beside the story feed, then the Sites tab sorted by experience with a Poor site at the top; flip to Remote Users to show the same scoring per person. [1.5 min]

  2. Drill one user to the failing node

    Home → Experience Monitoring User / site drill-down → Path Analysis

    Say: “Here is one user — Marcus, an office user with the Client on his laptop behind the site's Socket. His path is decomposed into nodes, each scored on its own against published thresholds. Everything is green except one. That is the fault domain: not the laptop, not the circuit, not Cato, not the app.”

    Show: the user's drill-down page, the score timeline with its connect and disconnect events, then the Connection Details node chain; hover the amber node for its averages. [1.5 min]

  3. Walk the path hop by hop

    User / site drill-down → Path Analysis

    Say: “When the amber sits on the last mile rather than the Wi-Fi, this is the next click — on the same office user, or on his site. Every hop from the device to the PoP, with per-hop loss and latency, kept historically. If the loss starts before the PoP it is the local network or the ISP; if it starts on the application route it is beyond Cato. Either way, here is the evidence.”

    Show: the site or office-user drill-down, then the Traceroute tab — the Last Mile Routes diagram with the hop that turns red before a clean Cato PoP — then the Command Line tab for the same route as a table. [1 min]

  4. Close on the proactive loop

    Home → Experience Monitoring Probes Home → Reports

    Say: “Everything so far was passive — real traffic, scored. On top of it you define ICMP, TCP, HTTP/S and DNS probes for the applications you care about, scoped by site or user group; the anomaly engine baselines each site-and-app pair and raises a story when it drifts; and the Site Experience report arrives on a schedule. The monthly service review writes itself.”

    Show: the probe policy with a Site rule and a User rule, then the Site Experience template in the Reports catalogue. If time is short, say it and skip the click. [1 min]

If running late

Per the cut plan: drop the Path Analysis drill (step 3) and show the score only — Beat 1 and the Connection Details chain in Beat 2 carry the proof point on their own. Never cut this segment if the audience is network- or operations-led; it is the one they came for.

Transition. Close the console and turn to the room with the bridge-out: “One console, seven capabilities, thirty-five minutes of screen time. What would you want to see proven on your own traffic?” Parked questions come first, then next steps and the PoV scope on the flow page.

If they ask

Four likely questions

“Do we need another agent for this?”

No — the Cato Client on the device and the Socket at the site already supply the telemetry, so there is nothing extra to deploy. The DEM pages and features do need a separate DEM licence, and the device, Wi-Fi and LAN gateway nodes need a recent Client on the endpoint. Licensing and prerequisites →

“How do you know it is the ISP and not your tunnel?”

The last mile is measured two ways: overlay probes inside the DTLS tunnel and underlay probes sent outside it, Socket-to-PoP or Client-to-PoP. When both show loss the circuit is the problem, not the tunnel — and Path Analysis shows which hop it starts at. Probes and path analysis →

“What are the scores actually based on?”

Application scores are judged against an AI baseline per application built from real traffic — Time to First Byte, TCP Connect, TLS Connect, HTTP latency and HTTP/S error rate — so a Europe-hosted app reached from Sydney is judged against what that path should deliver. Node scores use published thresholds, and scoring counts successful outbound TCP flows only. How each node is scored →

“Can it tell us before the users do?”

Yes — the Experience Anomaly engine baselines TTFB for each site-and-application pair over 14 days, then evaluates every day's traffic against it and raises stories with a criticality rating and playbook. Pair that with a recurring Site Experience report and the review arrives on a schedule, not on a ticket. Anomaly stories and reports →

For a remote worker's per-app drill-down in depth — the summary strip, the Select Application widget and the Devices panel — hand off to Remote Worker Experience.
